== Biography ==

'''Nicholas William Welch''' (1762–c. 1832), often identified in records as '''Nicholas Welch Jr.''', was born in 1762 in Mecklenburg (or Anson) County, North Carolina, to [[Welch-185|Major Nicholas Welch]] (c. 1738–1814) and [[Moore-51757|Elizabeth E. Moore]] (c. 1740–c. 1780-1782). He became the de facto "'''man of the family'''" on the American mainland during his father's prolonged exile following the Revolutionary War. While his father traveled between the Bahamas, London, Louisiana, and Tennessee seeking to rebuild the family's fortune, Nicholas William anchored the family in South Carolina for over thirty years before pioneering into newly opened Georgia territories in the 1820s. He died circa 1832 in Newton County, Georgia.

=== Early Life and Revolutionary War Upheaval ===

Nicholas William Welch was born in '''North Carolina''' during the turbulent years leading to the American Revolution. His father, [[Welch-185|Major Nicholas Welch]], was a prosperous landowner on Indian Creek in what became Lincoln County. The family's world collapsed when Major Welch chose loyalty to the British Crown.

'''The Revolutionary War Crisis (1780-1782):'''
* '''June 20, 1780''' - Major Nicholas Welch fought at the '''Battle of Ramsour's Mill''' as a Loyalist officer under Colonel Hamilton
* '''1780-1781''' - The family's property in North Carolina was confiscated by Patriot authorities
* '''May 1781''' - Eighteen-year-old Nicholas William fled with his mother Elizabeth and siblings to '''Savannah, Georgia''' as Loyalist refugees
* '''1782''' - The family evacuated to '''East Florida (St. Johns Town)''' after British evacuation of Savannah
* '''c. 1780-1782''' - His mother Elizabeth E. Moore died during the exile, likely from the hardships of refugee displacement

While his father served in the Royal British Army and later lived in exile in the '''Bahamas (1784-1786)''' and traveled to '''London (1786)''' to petition for compensation, young Nicholas William became the effective head of the family on the American mainland. This responsibility, thrust upon him at age 18-20, defined his role for the next five decades.

=== The "Man of the Family" on the Mainland ===

Nicholas William's position was unique among the Welch children. While his father pursued restitution through legal channels (ultimately receiving only £448 from the Loyalist Claims Commission) and his younger siblings were "'''dispersed... among such families as could be prevailed on to take them in'''" (per Major Welch's 1786 Memorial), Nicholas William remained the stable presence maintaining the family's foothold in the new United States.

'''His Responsibilities:'''
* Managing family affairs while his father was in the Bahamas and London (1784-1788)
* Establishing a new homestead in South Carolina after the North Carolina properties were lost
* Providing refuge for siblings during the family's diaspora
* Maintaining connections with the extended Moore family (his mother's relatives)
* Eventually administering estates and serving as a respected community member despite the family's Loyalist past

=== Marriage and the Hembree Connection ===

Circa '''1783''', in either the Spartanburg or Pendleton District of '''South Carolina''', Nicholas William married '''Margaret "Peggy" Hembree''' (c. 1764–before 1808). This marriage connected the Welch family to one of the most prominent Baptist pioneer families in the Carolina backcountry.

'''Margaret's Family Background:'''
* Daughter of '''David Hembree''' (1728–1809), a Baptist pioneer and prominent settler
* The Hembree family were part of the Baptist migration from Virginia through Granville County, North Carolina, into the South Carolina backcountry
* The Hembrees, unlike the Welches, were Patriots during the Revolution—this marriage represented reconciliation between former enemies

'''Known Children of Nicholas William and Margaret:'''
* [[Welch-14551|Nicholas Welch]] (b. 1786 Anderson, Pendleton County, SC - d. 1870 SC)
* Several other children (names and dates to be documented)
* At least one daughter survived to live with him in Georgia (1820 census records)

Margaret "Peggy" Hembree died '''before 1808''' in the Pendleton District, South Carolina, leaving Nicholas William a widower in his mid-40s.

=== South Carolina Period: Pendleton District (c. 1783–1815) ===

Nicholas William established himself as a respected landholder and community member in the '''Pendleton District''' of South Carolina (which later became Anderson, Pickens, and Oconee Counties). Despite his father's Loyalist past, he successfully rebuilt the family's reputation through decades of steady presence.

'''Land Holdings:'''
* Resided on '''26 Mile Creek''' in Pendleton District, near his wife's Hembree family
* '''1809''' - Filed plat for '''69 acres''' on 26 Mile Creek
* '''1811''' - Filed plat for '''113 acres''' in the same area
* Located in what is now '''Anderson County, South Carolina'''

'''Legal and Community Standing:'''
* '''1799''' - Administered the estate of '''Caleb Edmondson Jr.''' in Pendleton District, demonstrating trust and legal standing in the community
* This appointment is significant: only respected citizens were chosen as estate administrators, showing that his father's Loyalist stigma had not permanently tainted the family name

'''Census Records in South Carolina:'''
* '''1800 Pendleton District Census''' - Listed as head of household with wife Margaret and children
* '''1810 Pendleton District Census''' - Listed as widower over age 45 (Margaret having died before 1808)

'''The South Carolina Years (Summary):'''

For over '''thirty years''' (c. 1783–1815), Nicholas William Welch was the stable, mainland presence of the Welch family. While his father traveled between the Bahamas, London, South Carolina (briefly), Louisiana (land speculation ventures), and finally Tennessee (where he died in 1814), Nicholas William remained rooted in the Pendleton District, raising his family and managing his farm on 26 Mile Creek.

=== Migration to Georgia: Cherokee Lands (1818–1832) ===

Following the death of his father in 1814 and the conclusion of the War of 1812, Nicholas William joined the great westward migration into newly opened Georgia territories. The Cherokee Nation's forced cessions created opportunities for white settlers, and Nicholas William—now in his 50s—sought fresh land in the frontier.

'''Habersham County, Georgia (1818–1827):'''
* '''1818''' - Applied for land in the ceded '''Cherokee Territory''' of '''Habersham County, Georgia'''
* '''1820 Census''' - Recorded in Habersham County as a widower with one young daughter
* Age listed as 45-60 (consistent with birth year of 1762)

'''Newton County, Georgia (1827–1832):'''
* '''1827''' - Won a lot in the '''Cherokee Land Lottery''' and moved to '''Newton County, Georgia'''
* '''1830 Census''' - Listed in Newton County, age 60-70 (consistent with birth year 1762)
* '''c. 1832''' - Died in Newton County, Georgia

'''Second Marriage (Possible):'''

Some sources suggest Nicholas William married a '''second time circa 1822 to an unknown widow in Georgia'''. The 1820 census shows him as a widower with a daughter, but by the time of the 1827 land lottery, he may have remarried. This remains to be confirmed with primary sources.

=== Genealogical Distinction: Three Men Named Nicholas Welch ===

It is '''critically important''' to distinguish Nicholas William Welch (b. 1762) from other relatives bearing similar or identical names:

'''1. Nicholas William Welch (b. 1762)''' - '''THIS PROFILE'''
* Born 1762 Mecklenburg County, NC
* Son of [[Welch-185|Major Nicholas Welch]] and [[Moore-51757|Elizabeth E. Moore]]
* Married Margaret "Peggy" Hembree c. 1783
* Lived in Pendleton District, SC (1783-1815)
* Migrated to Habersham County (1818), then Newton County, GA (1827)
* Died c. 1832 Newton County, Georgia
* '''Father of''' [[Welch-14551|Nicholas Welch]] (b. 1786)

'''2. Nicholas Welch (b. 1786)''' - '''HIS SON''' - [[Welch-14551]]
* Born 1786 Anderson, Pendleton County, SC
* Son of Nicholas William Welch (b. 1762) and Margaret Hembree
* '''Grandson of''' [[Welch-185|Major Nicholas Welch]]
* Remained in South Carolina (Anderson County)
* Died 1870 South Carolina

'''3. Nicholas Welch Jr. (b. 1786)''' - '''HIS BROTHER OR COUSIN'''
* Born 1786 South Carolina
* Another son of [[Welch-185|Major Nicholas Welch]] (making him Nicholas William's younger brother)
* Became prominent '''Sheriff of Perry County, Tennessee'''
* '''NOT the same person as the Nicholas Welch who stayed in South Carolina'''

'''Confusion in Records:'''

Genealogists frequently confuse these three men because:
* All were born within the same generation (1762, 1786, 1786)
* All descended from [[Welch-185|Major Nicholas Welch]]
* Two share the exact name "Nicholas Welch" born in the same year (1786)
* Records often use "Nicholas Welch Jr." to refer to '''either''' the 1762-born son '''or''' the 1786-born Tennessee sheriff

'''The Correct Distinction:'''
* '''Nicholas William Welch (1762–1832 GA)''' = The eldest son, "man of the family," Pendleton District → Georgia pioneer
* '''Nicholas Welch (1786–1870 SC)''' = His son, remained in South Carolina
* '''Nicholas Welch Jr. (1786– TN)''' = His brother, became Sheriff in Tennessee

=== Historical Significance ===

Nicholas William Welch's life embodies the resilience and adaptation required of Loyalist refugee families in the post-Revolutionary period. Born into a prosperous North Carolina planting family, he spent his teenage years fleeing Patriot persecution, witnessed his mother's death in exile, and became the family's anchor while his father pursued compensation in London.

'''His Achievements:'''
* Successfully rehabilitated the family's reputation in South Carolina despite Loyalist stigma
* Built a 30-year presence in Pendleton District through steady farming and community service
* Appointed estate administrator (1799)—a mark of trust and respectability
* Pioneered into Georgia's Cherokee territories in his 50s-60s
* Bridge between his father's ruined Loyalist generation and his children's American generation

Unlike his father, who never fully recovered his fortune and died on the Tennessee frontier, Nicholas William found modest prosperity and community acceptance through patient, persistent work in one place. He represents the Loyalist descendants who quietly assimilated into the new American republic, their family's wartime allegiance gradually forgotten.

=== Research Notes ===

'''Birth Year (1761 vs. 1762):'''
Some sources list 1761, others 1762. The 1830 census (age 60-70) and 1820 census (age 45-60) both support birth year of '''1762'''.

'''Death Date:'''
Most sources agree on '''circa 1832 in Newton County, Georgia'''. Some older sources incorrectly state 1830 or 1822; these appear to confuse him with other Nicholas Welches.

'''Mother's Identity:'''
Nicholas William was almost certainly the son of '''Elizabeth E. Moore''' (the Major's wife who died c. 1780-1782), making him full brother to [[Welch-1890|Lewis Welch]] and other children born 1758-1780. He was '''NOT''' the son of Sarah Farren (whom Major Nicholas married in 1794 in Philadelphia after returning from London).

'''Margaret Hembree's Death:'''
She died '''before 1808''' (definitely before the 1810 census which lists Nicholas as a widower). The exact date and burial location remain unknown.

'''Larry Petrisky Research Note:'''
<blockquote>The following is an excerpt from Larry Petrisky's Hembree-Vann Connections website:
<br>"The Granville County, NC Connection:
<br>The Virginia Hembrees, Heatons, Rainwaters, Fowlers, Moselys, Kings, Days, and Meadows all resided in Granville County, NC, during the Baptist migration. So did the Welch family of Nicholas Welch (b.1761 NC d.1822TN) who married Margaret Hembree (d.bef.1810), daughter of David Hembree. These Welches are no relation to the mixed-blood family of John Welch, though they both had brothers Thomas and William Welch. In the 1754 militia rosters for Granville County, all these names are represented..."</blockquote>
