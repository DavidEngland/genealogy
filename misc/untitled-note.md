== ᎤᏃᏴᏫ (Introduction) ==

**ᏭᏖᎯ (Wu‑te‑he)** ᎠᏂᏬᏗ ᏗᏟᎶᏍᏗ ᎠᏂᏴᏫ ᎠᎴ ᎤᏃᏴᏫ ᎤᏓᎷᎸᏔᏅ ᎤᏓᏅᏙᏗ.
*Wu‑te‑he was born into the Ani‑Wodi (Paint Clan), one of the ancient matrilineal clans of the Cherokee.*

She lived in the Overhill towns near **ᎠᎹᏯ ᎢᏣ (Echota)** during the mid‑1700s, a period shaped by leaders such as **ᎼᏱᏙᎢ (Moytoy)** and **ᎣᎪᎾᏍᏔᏘ (Oconostota)**.

----

== ᎤᎾᏙᏢᏒ ᎠᎴ ᎤᏃᏴᏫ (Origins & DNA) ==

**ᎠᏂᏍᎩᏟ ᎠᏓᏅᏖᏗ (DNA Evidence)**
Recent genetic analysis reveals that ᏭᏖᎯ may have been born of **Eastern European Jewish ancestry** but was **raised fully Cherokee** within the **ᎠᏂᏬᏗ (Ani‑Wodi)** clan.

In the 18th‑century Overhill towns, adoption and integration of captives, traders' children, and refugees was a living practice. Identity was determined not by blood, but by **ᎤᏓᏅᏙᏗ (clan)**, **ᎧᏃᎮᏛ (community)**, and **ᎦᏚᎩ (gadugi / shared labor and responsibility)**.

**ᏭᏖᎯ ᏧᎴᎳᏍᏗ ᏣᎳᎩ.**
*Wu‑te‑he lived as Cherokee.*

----

== ᎤᏓᏅᏙᏗ ᎠᏂᏬᏗ (Clan Identity) ==

**ᎠᏂᏬᏗ (Ani‑Wodi / Paint Clan)**
ᎠᏂᏬᏗ ᎤᏃᏴᏫ ᎤᏓᎷᎸᏔᏅ ᎤᏓᏅᏙᏗ.
*She inherited her clan from her mother, as all Cherokee children do.*

The Paint Clan is traditionally associated with medicine, healing, and ceremonial color.

Regardless of her birth origins, Wu‑te‑he was **ᎠᏂᏬᏗ**, and her children inherited that clan through her.

----

== ᎤᏓᏅᏙᏗ ᎠᏂᏴᏫ (Family & Lineage) ==

**ᎠᏂᏣᎳᎩ (Her Children)**
Wu‑te‑he's descendants appear in later Overhill genealogies.
Because Cherokee society is **matrilineal**, her children belonged to **her** clan, not their father's.

**ᎠᏂᏍᎩᏟ ᎠᏓᏅᏖᏗ (DNA Evidence)** suggests her children may have had **different fathers**. This was not unusual in Cherokee society of the era, where:

* Women controlled household and family decisions
* Marriage customs differed greatly from European norms
* Serial relationships and plural partnerships existed
* **Clan membership through the mother** was the defining identity, not paternity

**ᏧᏂᎸᏫᏍᏓᏁᏗ ᏄᏓᎴᏅᏎᎸᎢ.**
*These customs should not be judged by modern standards.*

Her line continued through daughters and grandchildren who remained in the Little Tennessee River valley until the era leading to **ᏄᎾᎯ ᏚᎾᏙᎶᎯᎵᎩ (Nunahi‑Dunadlohiligi / Trail of Tears).**

----

== ᎤᏃᏴᏫ ᎤᏓᏅᏙᏗ (Historical Context) ==

Wu‑te‑he lived during a century of upheaval:

* **ᎼᏱᏙᎢ (Moytoy)** and shifting diplomacy
* **ᎣᎪᎾᏍᏔᏘ (Oconostota)** and Overhill leadership
* Frontier violence and encroachment
* Early tensions that later formed the **ᏥᎧᎹᎩ (Tsikamagi / Chickamauga)** towns

Though she lived before removal, her descendants endured its consequences.

----

== ᏣᎳᎩ ᎦᏬᏂᎯᏍᏗ (Language & Culture) ==

**ᏣᎳᎩ ᎦᏬᏂᎯᏍᏗ (Tsalagi Gawonihisdi / Cherokee language)**
ᏭᏖᎯ ᏣᎳᎩ ᎦᏬᏂᎯᏍᏗ ᎤᏃᏴᏫ ᎤᏓᏅᏙᏗ.
*She lived in a world shaped entirely by the Cherokee language.*

She lived before **ᏍᏏᏉᏯ (Sequoyah)** created the syllabary, but the oral language was strong, poetic, and central to identity.

**ᎦᏚᎩ (Gadugi)**—communal responsibility—guided daily life, agriculture, and diplomacy.

----

== ᎤᏃᏴᏫ ᎤᏓᏅᏙᏗ (Marriage & Social Role) ==

**ᎠᎨᏴ ᎠᏍᎦᏯ (Women's Authority)**
As an Ani‑Wodi woman, Wu‑te‑he held authority within her clan.
Cherokee women traditionally controlled:

* **ᎦᏙ (Gado / property)** — household possessions and dwellings
* **ᏎᎷ (Selu / agriculture)** — corn, beans, and cultivated fields  
* **ᎠᏂᏣᎳᎩ (Anitsalagi / children)** — upbringing and clan membership
* **ᏧᎾᏕᎶᏆᏍᏗ (Relationships)** — choice of partners

**ᏧᏂᎪᏗ ᎠᎴ ᏧᎾᏕᎶᏆᏍᏗ (Marriage and Relationships)**
Cherokee marriage in the 18th century was **not like European Christian marriage**. Women could:

* Form relationships with multiple partners over time
* Dissolve relationships by choice
* Retain full authority over children and property

Wu‑te‑he's relationships likely reflected **ᏣᎳᎩ ᏧᏂᎪᏗ (Cherokee custom)**, not colonial law or judgment.

Her partnerships may have strengthened ties between Overhill families, clans, and trading networks.

----

== ᎤᏃᏴᏫ ᎤᏓᏅᏙᏗ (Legacy) ==

Wu‑te‑he’s legacy survives through:

* **Ani‑Wodi clan identity**
* **Tsalagi language**
* **Gadugi values**
* **Matrilineal continuity**

Her descendants carried her name and clan through the Overhill region into the 19th century.

----

**Clan:** ᎠᏂᏬᏗ (Ani‑Wodi / Paint Clan)
**Language:** ᏣᎳᎩ ᎦᏬᏂᎯᏍᏗ (Cherokee)
**Culture:** Matrilineal; practiced ᎦᏚᎩ (Gadugi)
**Family:** DNA suggests children may have had different fathers (consistent with Cherokee custom)
**Historical Era:** Moytoy, Oconostota, early frontier conflict
**Legacy:** Continued through daughters and grandchildren in the Overhill towns
