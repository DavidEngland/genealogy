== Biography ==

'''Lewis Welch''' was born in 1780 in North Carolina to Nicholas Welch (c. 1738–1814) and Elizabeth E. Moore (c. 1740–c. 1780-1782). He lived one of the most dramatic generational transformations in American history: born into a Loyalist family torn apart by the Revolution, exiled as an infant to British territories in Florida and the Bahamas, and ultimately returning to fight '''for''' the United States in the War of 1812—the very nation his father had opposed.

=== Early Life: A Tory Refugee's Childhood ===

Lewis was born in 1780 in North Carolina, just as the Revolutionary War reached its brutal climax in the Carolina backcountry. As an infant, his life was shattered by the Patriot victory at the Battle of Ramsour's Mill (June 20, 1780), after which his mother Elizabeth and nine siblings were '''banished''' from North Carolina in October 1780. Lewis's childhood was that of a "Tory refugee," moving between British outposts:

  • '''1780–1781:''' Fled as refugee to Georgia with mother and siblings; family "stript of every thing they had, except only a small part of their wearing apparel"
  • '''1781–1783:''' Lived in '''East Florida''' at St. Johns Town (Hester's Bluff), where the family received a 500-acre land grant
  • '''1784–1786:''' Moved to '''New Providence, Bahamas''', living in "misery and want" while father Nicholas traveled to London alone to petition the Crown for compensation
  • '''Late 1780s:''' Family returned to the American mainland

During his father's absence in London (1785-1786), Lewis and his siblings were "dispersed... among such families as could be prevailed on to take them in." His mother Elizabeth likely died during this exile period (c. 1780-1782), adding to the family's suffering.

=== Rehabilitating the Family Name ===

Lewis was the generation that reconciled the Welch family with the United States. While his father had fought for King George III, Lewis (and his brother '''Zachariah Welch''') served in the '''War of 1812''' fighting '''for''' the American cause—a profound symbolic reversal that demonstrated the family's reintegration into the new Republic.

=== Migration Path ===

Lewis Welch's migration followed the classic frontier path, gradually moving westward as he reestablished the family in American society:

  • '''By 1790:''' Family appeared in '''Orangeburg, South Carolina''' after returning from the Bahamas
  • '''c. 1800:''' Residing in '''South Carolina''', where his son John C. Welch was born
  • '''By 1810:''' Moved to the "ever-expanding colony of Welches" in '''Hopkins County, Kentucky''', living next to Henry Welch
  • '''By 1820:''' Settled in '''Perry County, Tennessee''', helping establish the Welch lineage as a dominant administrative force in the Buffalo River valley

=== Presence in Perry County, Tennessee ===

Lewis Welch was a foundational pioneer in the early settlement of Perry County, Tennessee, part of an expansive Welch family presence that became influential in regional governance.

==== 1820 Census Record ====

The '''1820 Perry County census''' documents Lewis's household:Tennessee 1850 census records; research by Herb Perry

  • Known sons: '''John C. Welch''' (b. c. 1800 in South Carolina) and '''Elijah C. Welch''' (b. 1810 in Hopkins County, Kentucky)
  • One unknown son
  • Three females, one of whom is believed to be his wife (identity remains unknown)

==== Family Network in Perry County ====

Lewis was part of a coordinated Welch family settlement that dominated the region:

  • '''Zachariah Welch''' - Lewis's brother; fellow War of 1812 veteran who held a military land grant near the Wayne/Perry County line
  • '''Nicholas Welch Jr.''' (born 1786) - Possible relative; one of the vanguard settlers on '''White Oak Creek''' in Perry County between 1818 and 1820; later served as '''Sheriff of Perry County''' after 1830

This family network suggests coordinated settlement patterns common among War of 1812 veterans who utilized military land grants to establish communities in the Tennessee frontier.

=== War of 1812 Service ===

Lewis Welch served in the War of 1812 for the United States. Specific unit assignments and service dates require further documentation through:

  • National Archives, War of 1812 Compiled Service Records
  • Kentucky State Archives, War of 1812 pension applications
  • Perry County, Tennessee, land grant records (veterans received bounty land)

His service represents the reconciliation of the Welch family with the American Republic, fighting for the nation his father had opposed just one generation earlier.

=== Marriage and Family ===

Lewis married an unknown woman, whose identity remains one of the most significant research gaps in this family line. Through this marriage, he had at least the following children:

  • '''John C. Welch''' (b. c. 1800 in South Carolina)
  • '''Elijah C. Welch''' (1810–1867) - Born in Hopkins County, Kentucky; married '''Mary Taylor''' (1801–1880)
  • One additional son (name unknown, documented in 1820 census)

Lewis's son Elijah settled in the '''Wayne and Perry County region''' of Tennessee, continuing the family's frontier expansion.

=== Lineage to Serena Welch ===

Through his son '''Elijah C. Welch''' (1810–1867), Lewis became the grandfather of '''Serena Welch''' (c. 1833–1884). Serena's marriage to James Monroe Hargrove connected the Welch line (Loyalist heritage) to the family of Patriot Revolutionary War veteran John Washington Hargrove Sr., symbolizing the complete reconciliation of former enemy families on the Tennessee frontier.

=== Surname Variations ===

The surname appears in records as: Welch, Welsh, Wellch, and Welshe. Researchers should search under all variants to capture complete records.

== Research Notes ==

=== Unresolved Questions ===

  1. Identity of Lewis's wife - The most significant research gap for this profile; she may have died between 1810 (Elijah's birth) and 1820 (census shows only three females)
  2. Exact War of 1812 service details - Unit assignment, service dates, pension application
  3. Death date and location - Not documented in available sources; likely died after 1820 in Perry County or migrated elsewhere
  4. Identity of third son - Documented in 1820 census but name unknown
  5. Relationship to Nicholas Welch Jr. - Possibly a nephew or cousin; precise relationship needs confirmation
  6. Relationship to Zachariah Welch - Confirmed as brother in compiled sources

=== Recommended Records for Further Research ===

For Wife's Identity:

  • '''Hopkins County, Kentucky land deeds (1806–1820)''' - Look for dower rights signatures that might name her; wives were required to sign releases when husbands sold land
  • Hopkins County vital records (1806–1820)
  • Hopkins County church records - Presbyterian, Baptist, Methodist congregations
  • Hardin County, Tennessee records for son John C. Welch may contain biographical clues

For War of 1812 Service:

  • National Archives, War of 1812 Compiled Service Records
  • Kentucky State Archives, War of 1812 pension applications
  • Perry County land grant records for veterans
  • Tennessee War of 1812 soldier databases

For Perry County Settlement:

  • '''Lawrence County, Tennessee "Crockett Docket"''' - The Minute Book, Court of Pleas and Quarter Sessions, Lawrence County, TN, 1818–1822 (locally called the "Crockett Docket") contains approximately '''50 mentions of the Welch family''' during their arrival in the region. This is a critical primary source for early Welch settlement patterns.
  • Perry County, Tennessee deed records (1818–1830)
  • Perry County tax lists (1820–1830)
  • Perry County court minutes (estate settlements, guardianships)
  • Wayne County records for connections to son Elijah's settlement

For Family Connections:

  • DNA testing to identify maternal line through autosomal matches
  • Shared matches with Hopkins County Kentucky families (1800-1820 period)
  • North Carolina emigration records (1790-1810)
  • Orangeburg, South Carolina records (1786-1800)
