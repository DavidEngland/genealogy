== Biography ==
'''Elizabeth E. Moore''' (1740–c. 1780–1782) was born on March 19, 1740, in the Indian Creek area of Tryon County (now Lincoln County), North Carolina. Her parentage remains unresolved (see Research Notes below), but she was definitely part of the prominent Moore family of Loyalists in that region. She married '''Major Nicholas Welch''' (c. 1738–1814) in 1757 in North Carolina and bore at least nine children before her untimely death during the Loyalist diaspora following the American Revolution.

Elizabeth's life ended tragically during the family's forced exile from North Carolina. She died somewhere between her home colony and East Florida between 1780 and 1782, most likely from the hardships of refugee displacement. She was buried in an unmarked grave, her final resting place lost to history—one of the uncounted casualties of the Revolutionary War.

=== Marriage and Family ===

Elizabeth married '''[[Welch-185|Nicholas Welch]]''' in 1757, a colonial Pennsylvania landowner who later became a controversial Loyalist officer during the American Revolution. Together they had at least nine children documented by the time of their exile from North Carolina in 1780. 

'''The Children and Their Fates:'''

Of Elizabeth's nine children, only six are documented as surviving by the time Nicholas reached St. Johns Town, East Florida (1782–1784). These were likely the younger children. The fate of the other three is not documented in available sources. Adding to the genealogical complexity, Elizabeth had '''two daughters named Hester'''; one died very young, which has caused confusion in family records.

'''What Happened to the Children:'''
* Some sons are believed to have renounced the Crown
* Some daughters died or married before the exile
* At least two daughters married in the Bahamas: '''[[Welch-8473|Mary Welch]]''' (b. 1762) married James Misick in Nassau (1785) and remained in the Caribbean
* The exact composition and fates of the six children who accompanied Nicholas to Florida remain unclear in genealogical records

=== Life and Hardship ===

Elizabeth's life spanned a transformative and tragic period in American history. Born in colonial North Carolina, she married during the imperial era and witnessed the American Revolution as the wife of a Loyalist officer. As the wife of '''Major Nicholas Welch''', she endured the brutal consequences of the family's Loyalist allegiance: the arrest of her husband for treason, the loss of their 600-acre estate, the capture of their sons at Ramsour's Mill, and ultimately forced exile from their homeland.

Elizabeth died during the diaspora period between 1780 and 1782, most likely during the arduous journey from North Carolina to East Florida or shortly after arrival. By the time Nicholas Welch established residence in St. Johns Town, East Florida (1782–1784), historical records confirm he arrived as '''a widower with six children'''. Elizabeth likely died from the hardships of displacement—whether from disease, the rigors of refugee travel, complications of childbirth, or the accumulated trauma of losing everything. She never saw her husband's eventual return to America or the success of her descendants in Tennessee.

Her surviving children became prominent settlers in the Tennessee-Alabama frontier, including War of 1812 veterans, farmers, and frontier families who established communities in Hardin County, Tennessee, and surrounding regions—a legacy Elizabeth herself would never witness.

=== Revolutionary War and Exile (1780) ===

'''The Moore Family and Loyalist Cause:'''

Elizabeth belonged to the prominent Moore family of Tryon County (now Lincoln County), North Carolina. '''Captain Moses Moore''' (1718–1797), a likely kinsman, resided on Indian Creek. '''Lt. Colonel John "The Tory" Moore''' (1753–1781), likely Elizabeth's brother or close kinsman, commanded Loyalist forces in '''Hamilton's Tory Regiment'''. Elizabeth's husband, '''Major Nicholas Welch''', served as a Loyalist officer in the same regiment.

The '''Battle of Ramsour's Mill''' (June 20, 1780) ended in Patriot victory, becoming the first in a series of defeats culminating in '''King's Mountain''' (October 7, 1780). For Elizabeth and her family, the consequences were devastating.

'''Exile and Elizabeth's Death:'''

Following the British defeats, Elizabeth, Nicholas, and their surviving children were banished from North Carolina by Revolutionary authorities in October 1780.

* '''October 1780:''' Forced departure from Lincoln/Tryon County, North Carolina
* '''1780–1782:''' Southward trek through the Carolinas toward East Florida
* '''c. 1780–1782:''' '''Elizabeth dies''' during this period—likely from disease, hardship of refugee travel, or childbirth complications
* '''1782–1784:''' Nicholas arrives in '''St. Johns Town, East Florida''' as a widower with six surviving children

Historical records from the University of North Florida document that "Nicholas Welsh, a planter originally from New York, came to a house in St. Johns Town a widower with six children." This confirms Elizabeth died before reaching Florida.

'''Note on "New York" Origin:''' The Florida record listing Nicholas as "from New York" is a biographical error. Nicholas was actually born in '''Lancaster County, Pennsylvania''' (c. 1738), with family ties to neighboring '''York County, Pennsylvania'''. When providing his background to Florida officials, he likely mentioned "York"—referring to the Pennsylvania county—which was misinterpreted or transcribed as "New York," the more prominent colony. Genealogical research confirms Nicholas was part of the Pennsylvania → North Carolina → Florida migratory line, not a New York resident.

=== The Family After Elizabeth's Death ===

'''The Bahamas Years (1784–1786):'''

After Elizabeth's death, Nicholas relocated the surviving children to '''New Providence, Bahamas''' (1784). While there, the family permanently divided:

* '''[[Welch-8473|Mary Welch]]''' (b. 1762) married '''James Misick''' in Nassau (1785) and remained in the Caribbean, eventually settling in the Turks Islands where she died in 1822. Her descendants established the Misik family line, confirmed by DNA testing in the 21st century.
* The other surviving children eventually returned to America with their father.

'''Return to America (1786–1814):'''

Nicholas returned to the mainland by 1786 (son Nicholas Jr. born in Pendleton County, SC that year). The family migrated through South Carolina, Kentucky, and eventually Tennessee. Nicholas died in Giles County, Tennessee in December 1814. 

'''Estate Administration Confusion:''' There has been confusion about which "Sarah Welch" served as administratrix of Nicholas's estate. Court records show '''Sarah Welch, administratrix''' returned the estate inventory in December 1814. This was '''Sarah Farren Welch''' (whom Nicholas married in Philadelphia in 1794), not his daughter '''Sarah Welch''' (b. 1766). One source notes the widow qualified, though records state "to be recorded later," and no subsequent documentation has been located. Sarah Farren Welch lived to an advanced age (she died at 99 years old in 1851).

Elizabeth's surviving children became prominent Tennessee frontier settlers, War of 1812 veterans, and community founders across Giles, Lawrence, Perry, Wayne, Hardin, and Gibson counties—a legacy she never lived to witness.

=== Legacy ===

Elizabeth Moore Welch died during one of the most brutal displacements of the American Revolution. She witnessed her husband arrested for treason, her sons captured in battle, and her family's 600-acre estate confiscated before being forced into exile from her homeland. She died somewhere along the refugee trail between North Carolina and East Florida, buried in an unmarked grave.

Elizabeth never saw her husband's return to America, never witnessed Tennessee, and never knew her children would reestablish themselves as successful frontier settlers. Yet her legacy lived on: her descendants became War of 1812 veterans, community leaders, and foundational families across Tennessee's frontier counties.

She paid the ultimate price for her family's political allegiance—not killed in battle, but destroyed by its aftermath. She remains one of the uncounted casualties of the Revolutionary War.

=== Death and Burial ===

'''Elizabeth died between 1780 and 1782''' during the Loyalist diaspora. By 1782, Nicholas Welch is documented arriving in St. Johns Town, East Florida '''"a widower with six children,"''' confirming Elizabeth's death occurred before reaching Florida.

'''Burial:'''

The exact location of Elizabeth's burial is unknown. She likely received a funeral and burial marker at the time of her death, as was customary even during wartime displacement. However, the location has not been identified in available records.

'''The Giles County, Tennessee Confusion:'''

Find a Grave memorials list Elizabeth as buried in Giles County, Tennessee. However, this contradicts contemporary evidence showing Nicholas arrived in Florida (1782) as a widower. Evidence suggests the Giles County marker may actually be for '''Sarah Farren Welch''' (Nicholas's second wife, married 1794, died 1851 at age 99), possibly with commemoration for Elizabeth E. Moore. This would explain the persistent Giles County burial tradition while accounting for Elizabeth's actual death during the 1780-1782 diaspora.

'''What We Know:'''
* Elizabeth died somewhere between North Carolina and East Florida (1780-1782)
* She likely received a funeral and burial marker at that time
* The burial location has been lost to history
* Sarah Farren Welch (Nicholas's widow) is buried in Giles County, TN
* The Giles County marker attributed to Elizabeth may actually commemorate Sarah Farren, possibly with reference to Elizabeth

== Research Notes ==

=== Conflicting Parentage Information ===

Elizabeth E. Moore's parentage remains '''unresolved''' with multiple conflicting sources. The Moore family connections are confident, but the exact placement within the family tree requires further research:

'''Theory 1: Daughter of Lt. Col. John William Moore and Mary Susannah (Jouett) Moore'''

* '''Find A Grave''' Memorial #234157064 lists her parents as Lt. Col. John William Moore and Mary Susannah (Jouett) Moore
* '''WikiTree [[Moore-1641]]''' lists John William Moore (1706–1777) married to Mary Susannah Jouett, with daughter Elizabeth Moore (b. 1742) - '''but this is a different Elizabeth'''
* '''Conflict:''' WikiTree [[Moore-1640]] shows Elizabeth Moore (b. 1742) as their daughter, not Elizabeth E. Moore (b. 1740)

'''Theory 2: Daughter of Captain Moses Moore (1718–1797) and Hester Winston'''

* '''WikiTree [[Moore-60716]]''' identifies Moses Moore married to '''[[Winston-1414|Hester Winston]]'''
* '''FindAGrave data''' shows CPT Moses Moore (1718–1797) as parent of Captain John Moore, with siblings including:
**LTC John "The Tory" Moore (1753–1781) - commander at Ramsour's Mill
** Hester Moore Roberts (1762–1838)
** Sarah Moore Logan (d. 1840)
* Historical records confirm Moses Moore's residence on Indian Creek, eight miles west of Lincolnton, served as Loyalist mobilization headquarters (June 1780)
* '''Lt. Colonel John Moore''' (1753–1781), commander of Hamilton's Tory Regiment at Ramsour's Mill, fits as Elizabeth's brother in this scenario

'''Theory 3: Moses Moore from Carlisle, England'''

* Some sources identify her father as '''Moses Moore''', native of Carlisle, England, who migrated to Virginia (1745) before settling in North Carolina
* '''Question:''' Is this the same Moses Moore as CPT Moses Moore (1718–1797), or a different individual?

=== Key Questions Requiring Resolution ===

1. '''Which Moore line is correct?''' Is Elizabeth E. Moore the daughter of Lt. Col. John William Moore and Mary Susannah Jouett, or the daughter of CPT Moses Moore and Hester Winston?
2. '''Which Elizabeth Moore (b. 1740 vs. b. 1742)?''' WikiTree has two Elizabeth Moores in the same generation - one born 1740 (this profile), one born 1742 (Moore-1640).
3. '''The Two Hesters:''' Elizabeth had two daughters named Hester. One died very young. This has created significant confusion in genealogical records when trying to track which children survived the exile and which did not.
4. '''Which six children?''' Nicholas arrived in Florida with six children (likely the younger ones), but genealogical records don't definitively establish which six. Of the nine documented children, three did not survive to 1782—some sons may have renounced the Crown, some daughters may have died or married before the exile.
5. '''Where is Elizabeth buried?''' She likely received a funeral and burial marker during the diaspora (1780-1782), but the location has not been identified. Evidence suggests the Giles County, TN marker attributed to Elizabeth may actually be for Sarah Farren Welch (Nicholas's second wife), possibly with commemoration for Elizabeth.
6. '''DNA evidence:''' Autosomal DNA testing of confirmed descendants could help resolve which Moore line Elizabeth belongs to.

=== Uncertainty About First Wife Status ===

There is some uncertainty about whether Elizabeth Moore was Nicholas Welch's '''first''' wife:

* Nicholas later married '''Sarah Farren''' in Philadelphia in 1794
* Some genealogical records suggest there may have been an earlier marriage before Elizabeth, though documentation is lacking
* The large number of children (at least nine) and the 1757 marriage date support Elizabeth as the first wife
* Further research is needed to definitively establish whether Elizabeth was the first Mrs. Welch or if there was an earlier, undocumented marriage

=== Estate Administrator Identity ===

'''Which Sarah administered Nicholas Welch's estate?'''

Giles County court records (December 1814) show '''\"Sarah Welch, administratrix\"''' returned the estate inventory. This has been a source of confusion:

* '''It was Sarah Farren Welch''' (Nicholas's widow, married 1794), not his daughter Sarah Welch (b. 1766)
* One source indicates \"the widow qualifies\" to serve as administratrix
* Records note the estate was \"to be recorded later,\" but no additional documentation has been located
* Sarah Farren Welch lived to age 99 (d. 1851), which matches later records
* The confusion arose because both Nicholas's widow and his daughter shared the name Sarah Welch
=== The "New York" Error in Florida Records ===

The University of North Florida source describing Nicholas Welsh as "a planter originally from New York" contains a biographical error that has caused genealogical confusion.

'''The Actual Origin:'''
* Nicholas Welch was born in '''Lancaster County, Pennsylvania''' (c. 1738), not New York
* His family had ties to neighboring '''York County, Pennsylvania'''
* Research in the Nicholas Welch Compendium identifies Jacob Welch from York County, PA, as a member of the same church as Nicholas's grandfather William Welsh

'''The Source of the Error:'''
* When Nicholas provided his background to Florida officials at St. Johns Town (1782), he likely mentioned '''"York"''' (referring to York County, Pennsylvania)
* Colonial officials or later transcribers likely assumed he meant the '''Province of New York''', a much more prominent colony
* This misinterpretation was recorded and has persisted in historical documents

'''Confirmed Migration Pattern:'''
Genealogical evidence definitively places Nicholas in the '''Pennsylvania → North Carolina → Florida → Bahamas → South Carolina → Tennessee''' migratory corridor, not a New York origin. His 1769 administration of his father John Welch's estate in North Carolina further confirms the Pennsylvania roots.
=== Recommendation ===

'''DNA testing''' of confirmed descendants from both potential Moore lines (John William Moore/Mary Jouett line vs. Moses Moore/Hester Winston line) could definitively resolve Elizabeth's parentage. Y-DNA testing of male descendants and autosomal DNA comparison would be particularly valuable.
