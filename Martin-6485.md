== Biography ==
{{1776 Sticker|unit=Revolutionary War Service}}

'''Nathaniel Martin''' (September 15, 1758 – December 1848) was a Revolutionary War veteran who became a foundational settler in Wayne County, Tennessee, where he and his sons served as administrative stalwarts alongside the Scott and Pigg families.

=== Origins and Early Life ===

Nathaniel Martin was born on September 15, 1758, with his birthplace documented in various sources as either New Hampshire or London, England.<ref name="revwar">Revolutionary War biography, Nathaniel Martin</ref> This discrepancy reflects the common challenges of tracing colonial-era origins and warrants further genealogical research.

=== Revolutionary War Service ===

Nathaniel Martin served in the Revolutionary War and was wounded at the Battle of Trenton, one of the pivotal early battles of the Revolution. He was captured during the battle and held as a prisoner of war until his release in 1786.<ref name="revwar" />

=== Migration to Tennessee ===

Following the Revolutionary War, Nathaniel Martin migrated to Wayne County, Tennessee, where he established a farm in the Bear Creek and Pinhook regions. He became an influential community member, and the Martin family developed close ties with other prominent families—particularly the Pigg and Lawson families—through land transactions, civic service, and intermarriage.<ref name="chronicles">Chronicles of the Bear Creek Pioneers</ref>

=== Family ===

Nathaniel Martin married '''Francis Turner'''.

==== Extended Family Connections ====

The Martin family proliferated extensively in Wayne County. By the 1891 Wayne County Census, District 8 included multiple Martin households with heads of household including Ben, James, Buck, Henry, Scott, and Tom Martin.<ref name="census1891">Wayne County, Tennessee Census, 1891, District 8</ref>

Other family members documented in Wayne County records include:

* '''Malinda Jane Martin''' (1858–1902) – married Jeremiah Henderson Pigg in 1878, strengthening the Martin-Pigg family alliance<ref name="martin_pigg">Wayne County Marriage Records, 1878</ref>
* '''Sally Martin''' – died at age 93 in 1909 in the Butlers Creek area<ref name="sally">Wayne County death records, 1909</ref>
* '''Ella Martin''' – died at age 20 in 1908 in the Butlers Creek area<ref name="ella">Wayne County death records, 1908</ref>

=== Wayne County Leadership ===

Nathaniel Martin and his sons—Henry, John William, and Andrew Jackson Martin—were deeply involved in Wayne County's civic life. They frequently served on juries and held various administrative positions, working alongside other prominent families such as the Scotts, Piggs, and Lawsons to establish the legal and governmental framework of the young county.<ref name="chronicles" />

The Martin family's multi-generational alliances with the Pigg and Lawson families through marriages, land transactions, and civic service created a network of influential families that shaped Wayne County's development throughout the 19th century.

=== Death and Estate Settlement ===

While some biographical sketches suggest Nathaniel Martin died around 1850, official court records confirm he died shortly before December 4, 1848, when the settlement of his estate was filed in Wayne County, Tennessee. The estate was managed by administrators John N. Gillis and James W. Stanfield.<ref name="estate" />

The estate settlement documents show that James Allen Lawson (husband of Mary Ann "Polly" Martin) was a primary participant, making three separate payments to the estate totaling $35.80. The settlement also included $7.70 for "articles for widow allowed by Commissioners," confirming that Francis Turner Martin survived her husband and received a widow's allowance.<ref name="estate" />

=== Land Legacy ===

The Martin family's land holdings played a crucial role in Wayne County's development, particularly in the Bear Creek and Pinhook regions. According to the ''Chronicles of the Bear Creek Pioneers'', the farm originally settled by Nathaniel Martin (the Martin Homestead) passed through the Sinclair family and was eventually purchased by his son-in-law, James Allen Lawson.

Another farm settled by Andrew Jackson Martin in the Pinhook Community remained in the Martin family until around 1905, when it passed to James M. Lawson, grandson of James Allen Lawson. These land transfers demonstrate the interconnected nature of frontier property management and the continuation of the Martin family legacy through Lawson descendants.<ref name="chronicles" />

James Allen Lawson's purchase of Nathaniel Martin's original homestead, combined with his substantial estate settlement payments ($35.80), provides strong documentary evidence of the close father-in-law/son-in-law relationship and the family's strategy of consolidating landholdings within kinship networks.
