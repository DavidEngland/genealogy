== Biography ==

'''Serena Welch''' was born circa 1833 in Tennessee<ref>"United States Census, 1850," index and images, ''FamilySearch'' (https://familysearch.org/pal:/MM9.1.1/MCDN-FW7 : accessed 05 Sep 2013), Serena Welch in entry for Elijah C Welsh, Hardin County, Tennessee, 1850.</ref> to [[Welch-1883|Elijah C. Welch]] (1810–1867) and Mary Taylor (1801–1880). She married [[Hargrove-279|James Monroe Hargrove]] and settled in Hardin County, Tennessee, where she raised her family until her death in 1884.

=== A Lineage Shaped by the Tory Diaspora ===

What initially appeared to be a simple North Carolina-to-Tennessee westward migration pattern reveals a far more complex story shaped by the '''Loyalist exile''' known as the '''Tory Diaspora'''. Serena's family history spans three generations of dramatic reconciliation with the American Republic:

==== Great-Grandfather: [[Welch-185|Major Nicholas Welch]] (c. 1738–1814) ====

Serena's great-grandfather was '''Major Nicholas Welch''', a Loyalist officer who fought for King George III during the American Revolution.<ref>Memorial of Nicholas Welsh, 1786. Great Britain, Public Record Office, Treasury, Class I, Volume 629, folios 391-392. Available at: http://www.royalprovincial.com/Military/mems/nc/clmwel.htm</ref> His service at the Battle of Ramsour's Mill (June 20, 1780) resulted in catastrophic consequences: defeat, banishment from North Carolina, and the '''forced exile of his entire family''' to British territories in East Florida and the Bahamas.<ref>Nicholas Welch Memorial to the Crown, 1786, detailing family banishment: "they were turned out of their habitation and stripped of every thing they had, except only a small part of their wearing apparel."</ref>

Nicholas's wife [[Moore-51757|Elizabeth E. Moore]] died during the diaspora period (c. 1780-1782), leaving their children dispersed among refugee families.<ref>UNF Florida History Online, "British East Florida 1763-1783," noting Nicholas Welch arrived in St. Johns Town "a widower with six children." https://www.unf.edu/floridahistoryonline/Projects/British.html</ref> Nicholas traveled to London in 1785-1786 to petition the Crown for compensation, receiving testimony from '''Lord Cornwallis himself'''—the British Commander-in-Chief who surrendered at Yorktown—along with Lord Rawdon and three other British officers.<ref>Memorial of Nicholas Welsh, 1786, Great Britain Public Record Office, Treasury Class I, Vol. 629, folios 391-392.</ref> He received £448 (only 39% of his claim), likely reduced because he attempted to claim two enslaved men as "losses."<ref>Nicholas Welch Memorial analysis, noting discrepancy between £1,150 claim and £448 award.</ref>

By the late 1780s, Nicholas made the extraordinary decision to '''return to the United States'''—the nation that had declared him a traitor—and reinvent himself as a land speculator operating from '''Charleston, South Carolina'''.<ref>"Lincoln County, North Carolina Real Estate Conveyance Records 1793–1800," ''FamilySearch'' (https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-998W-3WK4), January 1796 deed showing Nicholas Welch living in Pendleton County, South Carolina.</ref>

==== Grandfather: [[Welch-1890|Lewis Welch]] (b. 1780) ====

Serena's grandfather '''Lewis Welch''' was born in 1780 in North Carolina, just as the Revolution reached its violent climax.<ref>"Tennessee 1850 census records; research by Herb Perry" cited in Welch-1890 WikiTree profile.</ref> As an infant, Lewis became a '''Tory refugee''', exiled with his mother and siblings to:

* '''East Florida''' (St. Johns Town/Hester's Bluff, 1781-1783) — 500-acre land grant<ref>Nicholas Welch Memorial, 1786, noting family's St. Johns Town settlement.</ref>
* '''Bahamas''' (New Providence, 1784-1786) — Living in "misery and want" while Nicholas petitioned in London<ref>Nicholas Welch Memorial, 1786, describing family circumstances in Bahamas.</ref>

The family returned to the American mainland by the late 1780s, settling initially in '''Orangeburg, South Carolina'''.<ref>1790 U.S. Census records showing Nicholas Welch in South Carolina.</ref>

Lewis's migration path represents the gradual reintegration of the Welch family into American society:

* '''By 1810:''' Migrated to '''Hopkins County, Kentucky'''<ref>Birth location of Elijah C. Welch (1810) documented in 1850 census: Hopkins County, Kentucky.</ref>
* '''By 1820:''' Settled in '''Perry County, Tennessee''', alongside brothers Zachariah and other Welch family members<ref>"Minute Book, Court of Pleas and Quarter Sessions, Lawrence County, TN, 1818–1822" (the "Crockett Docket"), containing approximately 50 mentions of the Welch family during their settlement period.</ref>

Most remarkably, Lewis served in the '''War of 1812''' fighting '''for the United States'''<ref>War of 1812 service records; cited in Welch-1890 WikiTree profile.</ref>—the very nation his father had opposed—demonstrating the complete reconciliation of the Welch family with the American Republic.

==== Father: [[Welch-1883|Elijah C. Welch]] (1810–1867) ====

Serena's father '''Elijah C. Welch''' was born in 1810 in '''Hopkins County, Kentucky'''<ref>"United States Census, 1850," database with images, ''FamilySearch'' (https://familysearch.org/pal:/MM9.1.1/MCDN-FWS : accessed 06 Sep 2013), Elijah C Welsh, Hardin County, Tennessee, showing birth in Kentucky.</ref> to Lewis Welch and an unknown mother. Elijah married '''Mary Taylor''' (1801–1880), born in South Carolina, and migrated to '''Hardin County, Tennessee''', where he established himself as a farmer.

Together, Elijah and Mary had at least three children:

* '''[[Welch-1878|Serena Welch]]''' (c. 1833–1884) — married [[Hargrove-279|James Monroe Hargrove]]
* '''Albert S. Welch''' (b. 1838)
* '''Mary Ann Welch''' (b. 1848)

The 1850 Hardin County census lists Elijah as head of household with Mary and their daughter Serena.<ref>"United States Census, 1850," database with images, ''FamilySearch'' (https://familysearch.org/pal:/MM9.1.1/MCDN-FWS : accessed 06 Sep 2013), Elijah C Welsh, Hardin County, Tennessee.</ref> By 1860, the household included their younger children.<ref>"United States Census, 1860," database with images, ''FamilySearch'' (https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M8TD-M2Q : accessed 08 Jul 2024), Elijah Welch and Mary Welch, Hardin County, Tennessee.</ref>

Elijah died in 1867 in Hardin County during the Reconstruction period, survived by Mary, who lived until 1880.

=== Marriage and Family ===

Serena married '''[[Hargrove-279|James Monroe Hargrove]]''', uniting two families whose histories reflected opposite sides of the American Revolution:

* '''The Welch line''' descended from Loyalist Major Nicholas Welch (fought for Britain)
* '''The Hargrove line''' descended from Patriot [[Hargrove-261|John Washington Hargrove Sr.]] (fought for American independence)

Their marriage symbolized the complete reconciliation of former enemy families on the Tennessee frontier, three generations after the Revolution.

=== Life in Tennessee ===

Serena appears in the 1850 census as a 17-year-old living with her parents in Hardin County.<ref>"United States Census, 1850," FamilySearch, Serena Welch in entry for Elijah C Welsh, Hardin County, Tennessee.</ref> After her marriage to James Monroe Hargrove, she appears in subsequent census records as part of his household:

* '''1860:''' Listed in Hardin County with husband James M. Hargroves<ref>"United States Census, 1860," database with images, ''FamilySearch'' (https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M8TD-MLN : 26 July 2017), James M Hargroves, Hardin County, Tennessee.</ref>
* '''1870:''' Enumerated in Tennessee with husband James Hargroves<ref>"United States Census, 1870," database with images, ''FamilySearch'' (https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:MDD7-3B6 : 12 April 2016), James Hargroves, Tennessee, United States; citing p. 18, family 116, NARA microfilm publication M593.</ref>

=== The True Migration Pattern ===

The Welch family's movement was '''not''' a simple westward migration from North Carolina to Tennessee. Rather, it followed the complex path of '''Loyalist exile and gradual reintegration''':

'''1780-1786: Tory Diaspora'''
* North Carolina → Banishment (October 1780) → East Florida (1781-1783) → Bahamas (1784-1786) → London petition (Nicholas alone, 1785-1786)

'''1786-1800: Return and Rehabilitation'''
* Return to American mainland → South Carolina (Orangeburg, then Charleston/Pendleton region)

'''1800-1820: Frontier Expansion'''
* South Carolina → Hopkins County, Kentucky (by 1810) → Perry County, Tennessee (by 1820)

'''1820-1850: Settlement in Tennessee River Valley'''
* Perry County → Giles County → Lawrence County → Hardin County → Wayne County (various Welch branches)

This was a '''refugee family's journey''' back into American society, not a typical pioneer migration. Each generation moved westward while gradually rebuilding status and land ownership lost through Loyalist banishment.

=== Death ===

Serena Welch died in 1884 at approximately age 51.
