Olive Family History

Critical Clarification: Two Different Women Named Sarah

This document addresses considerable genealogical confusion regarding two distinct women named Sarah who married men named James Olive, both dying on the same date in the same county:

1. Sarah "Sally" Hartsfield Olive

  • Birth: 1787, North Carolina
  • Death: 11 January 1878, Lauderdale County, Alabama
  • Married: James Olive (son of Jesse Olive and Monicah Massey)
  • Marriage Date: 16 April 1812, Wake County, North Carolina

2. Sarah Meriwether Olive

  • Birth: ca. 1776, Amherst County, Virginia
  • Death: 11 January 1878, Lauderdale County, Alabama
  • Married: James Olive (son of Anthony Olive and Kerrenhappuch Hendon, b. 13 March 1785)
  • Marriage Date: 24 December 1808

Both women died on the same date (11 January 1878) in Lauderdale County, Alabama, creating significant genealogical confusion that has persisted for decades.

Migration and Later Life

James Olive died ca. 1830 in Bedford County, North Carolina. After his death, Sally (Hartsfield) Olive relocated to Lauderdale County, Alabama, likely as part of the larger Hartsfield family migration to the Paris, Tennessee area.

Note: There are accounts of a cholera outbreak during the migration, with families camping at a log church near Columbia, Tennessee (later rebuilt by slaves of the James Polk Plantation). Several family members died during this outbreak, possibly including James Olive, though this has not been conclusively proven.

Geographic Mobility and Possible Remarriages

Research Finding: Richard and Sarah Olive were documented living in southern Alabama, approximately halfway between Florence (The Shoals area) and Mobile—a considerable distance south from Lauderdale County.

Hypothesis: Given the geographic distance and time span (James Olive died 1858; Sarah died 1878), Sarah may have been widowed and remarried one or more times before her final residence in Lauderdale County. This would explain:

  1. The southern Alabama location
  2. The 20-year gap between James's death and her own
  3. Possible connections to different family networks

Age Note: If Sarah was born ca. 1776 and died in 1878, she would have been approximately 102 years old at death. While not impossible, this remarkable longevity warrants further verification of birth date.

The James Olive (1713–1805) Line: Origin of Both Families

Overview

Both Sarah Hartsfield Olive and Sarah Meriwether Olive married descendants of James Olive (1713–1805), who is the common ancestor of numerous Olive families in North Carolina, Georgia, Alabama, Tennessee, and beyond.

Important Note: Distinction from Olliff/Oliff Lines

The authors of "Olliff Family History" concluded that the James Olive (1713) line is NOT connected to the John Shears Olliff line, despite similarities in spelling and geography. The Olliff line likely originated from the "Oliff" families of Richmond and Westmoreland Counties, Virginia, separated from the Olive line by the Rappahannock River.

While descendants of John Shears Olliff sometimes used the "Olive" spelling (particularly in pension applications), genealogical researchers have documented the James Olive descendants separately and found no connection point.

Research Notes and Clarifications

The "Olive" vs. "Olliff" Spelling Question

Extensive research by Olliff family historians concluded that the James Olive (1713) line is NOT connected to the John Shears Olliff line, despite:

  • Geographic proximity (both in Virginia, both in North Carolina)
  • Similar spelling variations
  • Use of "Olive" spelling by some Olliff descendants in official documents

The Olliff line likely originated from the "Oliff" families of Richmond and Westmoreland Counties, Virginia, separated from the Gloucester County Olive line by the Rappahannock River.

Joseph Olliff (son of John Shears Olliff) signed pension applications as "Olliff" even though the documents used "Olive" spelling throughout. This has led to considerable confusion but does not indicate a connection between the lines.

Source: "Olliff Family History," Section 9-305

Connection to Other Family Lines

Researchers contributing to Olive family genealogy discovered unexpected connections to other prominent families:

  • Bryan Family: Olive (Hotchkiss) Cartwright and John Dorroh both descend from John Bryan and Sarah Bryan of Rowan County, North Carolina
  • Shelton Family: Mildred Olive descends from John Shelton (b. 1780s) and Catherine (Messer) Shelton

Hartsfield Connections

The Hartsfield family intermarried extensively with the Olive family:

  • Sarah "Sally" Hartsfield married James Olive (son of Jesse)
  • Multiple children of Anthony Olive married Hartsfields:
    • Mary Olive m. Henry Hartsfield
    • Hendon Olive m. Sarah Hartsfield
    • Elizabeth Olive m. Henry Hartsfield
    • Nancy Olive m. James Hartsfield

The "Hartsfield settlement" near Paris, Tennessee was a destination for multiple Olive family members migrating from North Carolina.
