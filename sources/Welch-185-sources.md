## Revolutionary War & Loyalist Claims

**Source:** Memorial of Nicholas Welsh to the Lords Commissioners of His Majesty's Treasury  
**Date:** March 20, 1786, London  
**Archive:** Great Britain, Public Record Office, Treasury, Class I, Volume 629, folios 391-392  
**Online:** [Royal Provincial Corps - Loyalist Journal](http://www.royalprovincial.com/Military/mems/nc/clmwel.htm)

**Content:**
- Nicholas's own account of service and losses
- Claim for £1,150 compensation (awarded £448, 39%)

  1. [Lord Cornwallis](http://www.royalprovincial.com/Military/mems/nc/clmwel2.htm) (March 16, 1786)

## Battle of Ramsour's Mill (June 20, 1780)

**Primary Source:** "Sketches of Western North Carolina Illustrating Principally the Revolutionary Period of Mecklenburg, Rowan, Lincoln and Adjoining Counties" [database on-line]

**Account:** Nicholas Welch described appearing in "brilliant red uniform," exhibiting "a considerable quantity of gold coins," and using his skills as a "great orator" to recruit 1,300 Loyalists. Three of his sons (Thomas, William, Nicholas Jr.) captured as prisoners after Patriot victory.

**Secondary Sources:**
- North Carolina Highway Historical Marker Program, Marker O-3
- "History of the Battle at Ramsour's Mill," Lincoln County Historic Properties Commission
- William S. Powell, ed., *Encyclopedia of North Carolina* (UNC Press, 2006)
- [Carolana.com - Battle of Ramsour's Mill](https://www.carolana.com/NC/Revolution/revolution_battle_of_ramseors_mill.html)

## East Florida Period (1782–1784)

**Source:** University of Florida, Florida History Online, "St. Johns Town"  
**Posted:** 06 Sep 2013 by MelaneyWelch

**East Florida Claims Commission testimony:**
- Nicholas Welsh (spelling variant) arrived at St. Johns Town **as a widower with six children**
- Holdings: house, nine enslaved laborers, warehouse lot, 500-acre land grant from Governor Patrick Tonyn
- Testimony from John Russell describing flourishing settlement before 1783 Spanish cession
