== Biography ==
{{1776 Sticker|unit=Patriotic Service, North Carolina, American Revolution}}
{{Society Ancestor|society=DAR|ancestor=A036765}}

'''William England''' (c. 1710/1720 – post 1790) was a Revolutionary War patriot who operated an iron furnace in Chatham County and Burke County, North Carolina. He is recognized as DAR Patriot Ancestor #A036765.

=== Origins and Early Life ===

The exact origins of William England remain a matter of genealogical debate:

* '''Ireland Theory:''' According to research by Brad Spencer, William was born circa 1710 in Donnell, Ireland, and may have changed his surname to "England" after immigrating to America. This theory suggests an Irish origin for the family name.<ref name="spencer">Source: Brad Spencer research, as cited by Janice Hardin</ref>

* '''Pennsylvania Birth:''' The DAR Genealogical Research System records his birth as circa 1720 in Pennsylvania.<ref name="dar">{{DAR-grs|A036765|William England|May 14, 2021}}</ref>

According to historian Ethelene Dyer Jones, William England migrated to America in 1733 and first settled in Pennsylvania.<ref name="jones">Ethelene Dyer Jones, "Revolutionary War Patriots: William England and son Daniel England," published article</ref> This migration date would be consistent with either origin theory.

Further research is needed to reconcile these accounts. It is possible that William was born in Ireland to a family that immigrated to Pennsylvania during his childhood, or that one of these sources refers to a different generation.

=== Migration and Settlement ===

After arriving in Pennsylvania in 1733, William England later moved his family to Maryland, where his son Daniel was born in 1752. The family subsequently relocated to Chatham County, North Carolina, where three more sons—Joseph, John, and Samuel—were born.<ref name="jones" />

=== Revolutionary War Service ===

William England provided patriotic service during the American Revolution by operating an iron furnace in North Carolina. He went into partnership with his brother-in-law, John Wilcox, and built an iron foundry on Hunting Creek near Morganton, North Carolina. The iron furnace cast cannons and cannon balls used in the war effort during the Revolution.<ref name="jones" />

Burke County, North Carolina was specifically selected for iron foundry operations due to its rich iron resources, which were critically needed for cannon ball production during the Revolution. The Wilcox family were Catholics who had supplied paper to Benjamin Franklin, and their partnership with William England represented an important contribution to the patriot cause.

His son Daniel England worked with his father in the foundry and applied for and received deferment from induction into the military because of his essential work at the foundry. Both William and Daniel were recognized by the Daughters and Sons of the American Revolution for their material assistance in the Revolutionary War effort.<ref name="jones" />

William is documented in the '''Roster of Revolutionary Soldiers in Georgia Vol. II''' (image 60, p. 48) and the '''Roster of N.C. Rev. Soldiers''' compiled by the D.A.R.<ref name="dar-roster">Roster of Revolutionary Soldiers in Georgia Vol. II. E (image 60. p. 48); Roster of N.C. Rev. Soldiers, by D.A.R.; DAR Patriot Index A036765</ref>

Children:
* '''Daniel England''' (1752, Maryland – 1818, Burke County, North Carolina) – married Margaret Guinn/Gwynn (1758, North Carolina – 1847, Georgia)<ref name="dar" /><ref name="jones" />
* '''Joseph England''' – born in Chatham County, North Carolina<ref name="jones" />
* '''John England''' – born in Chatham County, North Carolina<ref name="jones" />
* '''Samuel England''' – born in Chatham County, North Carolina<ref name="jones" />

By 1790, William was living in Fayetteville (Cumberland County), North Carolina, with his wife and six children, as recorded in early census records.<ref name="sar">U.S. Sons of the American Revolution Membership Applications, 1889-1970, #5</ref>

==== Descendants ====

Daniel England's wife, Margaret Guinn, received land in the Georgia land lottery and moved to Habersham County, Georgia with some of her children, including Richard England (1770–1835), Nancy, Rachel, and Deborah. Richard married Patsy Montgomery and owned land on which gold was found in Habersham (later White) County, Georgia. Richard was buried in the England Family Cemetery near the Chattahoochee River in present-day Helen, Georgia. Margaret moved to Choestoe in Union County, Georgia, where hers was reportedly the first grave in the Old Choestoe Cemetery.<ref name="jones" />

Richard England's son, Jonathan England (known as "Athan"), married Nancy Ingram, granddaughter of John Ingraham, a Revolutionary War soldier, continuing the family's military heritage into the next generation.<ref name="jones" />

=== Later Life and Death ===

William England was still living in Cumberland County, North Carolina after 1790. The exact date and place of his death are not confirmed in available records.

=== Research Notes ===

This William England line represents one branch of the England family that migrated from the mid-Atlantic region into North Carolina and eventually to Georgia. The family's migration pattern shows movement from Pennsylvania (1733) to Maryland to Chatham County, North Carolina, and then to Burke County, North Carolina, with descendants settling in Habersham County (later White County) and Union County, Georgia. Some branches intermarried with Cherokee families. Researchers should note that the family name appears in various spellings in historical records, and that "Guinn" and "Gwynn" appear to be variant spellings of the same surname for Margaret, Daniel's wife.

The family's involvement in iron production and foundry work represents an important contribution to early American industry and the Revolutionary War effort. The partnership with the Catholic Wilcox family, who supplied paper to Benjamin Franklin, demonstrates the diverse coalition of patriots who supported American independence. The discovery of gold on Richard England's land in Habersham County connects the family to Georgia's early gold rush era.

William England Jr.'s connection to the Duckworth family warrants further research using the Duckworth papers.

DNA analysis and further genealogical research may help clarify the Ireland vs. Pennsylvania origin question and establish firmer connections between this William England and other England family lines in the region.
