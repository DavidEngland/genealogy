## THE SIX CHILDREN AT HESTER'S BLUFF: THE CENTRAL QUESTION

### What We Know With Certainty

**East Florida Claims Commission Testimony (1782):**
> "Nicholas Welsh, a planter originally from New York, came to a house in St. Johns Town **a widower with six children**."

**Critical Facts:**
- Nicholas arrived at **St. Johns Town (Hester's Bluff)** at mouth of St. Johns River, East Florida in **1782**
- He was already a **widower** - Elizabeth E. Moore had died between 1780-1782
- He had **exactly six children** with him
- He received a **500-acre land grant** from Governor Patrick Tonyn
- He owned nine enslaved laborers, a lot with a warehouse, tools and cattle
- St. Johns Town was the **second most populous town** in East Florida (1782-1784)

### The "New York" Error Explained

The record says Nicholas was "originally from New York" - **this is an error**:
- Nicholas was born in **Lancaster County, Pennsylvania** (c. 1738)
- His family had ties to **York County, Pennsylvania**
- When providing his background to Florida officials, he likely mentioned **"York"** (the Pennsylvania county)
- Colonial officials assumed he meant the **Province of New York** and recorded it incorrectly
- Confirmed migration: Pennsylvania → North Carolina → Florida → Bahamas → South Carolina → Tennessee

## THE FLORIDA PERIOD (1782-1784)

### St. Johns Town (Hester's Bluff)

**Location:** Mouth of St. Johns River, East Florida  
**Developer:** Thomas Williamson  
**Population:** Second most populous town in East Florida (1782-1784)

**Nicholas's Holdings:**
- House in St. Johns Town
- Lot with a warehouse
- **Nine enslaved laborers**
- Tools and cattle
- **500-acre land grant** from Governor Patrick Tonyn

**The Town:**
- 230 residential and commercial lots platted
- Roads laid out
- 100-300 dwellings built (estimates vary)
- Inns, taverns, shipyard, blacksmith shops
- "Very flourishing way, convenient for trade"

### Why It Failed

**1783:** Britain ceded East Florida to **Spain** in Treaty of Paris  
**Result:** Loyalist refugees faced **another displacement**

**Thomas Williamson (developer):**
- Dismantled his personal dwelling
- Loaded it on barges
- Shipped it to Jamaica (1784)
- Called sale price of £172 "the sole satisfaction or compensation" for his losses

**John Russell (fellow resident) testimony:**
> "the Town was in a very flourishing way, convenient for trade, and, from the extent of the river traffic must have become a Town of great consequence to the Country in a short time."

**Nicholas's situation:**
- Lost his Florida land grant when Spain took control
- **Third displacement in four years**: NC → GA/FL → Bahamas
- Had to relocate the six children again

## THE LONDON TRIP (1786)

### Nicholas's Journey to England

**Why:** To petition the Crown for compensation for his losses  
**When:** 1785-1786  
**Where:** London, presenting Memorial to Lords Commissioners of His Majesty's Treasury

### The Memorial (March 20, 1786)

**Nicholas's Claim:**
- **£1,150 sterling** for losses

**What he lost:**
- **600 acres** of developed land in North Carolina
- **Two valuable enslaved men** (highly skilled)
- All personal property except clothing
- Military resignation: £150
- Legal fees for arrests: £150

**The Witnesses - EXTRAORDINARY:**

Nicholas received testimony from **FIVE British officers**:

1. **Lord Cornwallis** ⭐ - The British Commander-in-Chief who surrendered at Yorktown
2. **Lord Rawdon** - Major British commander
3. **Lt. Col. Hamilton** - Nicholas's direct commanding officer
4. **Lt. Col. McArthur** - British officer
5. **Lt. Col. Brown** - British officer

**This is remarkable:** Lord Cornwallis himself testified on Nicholas's behalf **five years after surrendering at Yorktown**. This shows Nicholas was a significant Loyalist officer, not just minor militia.

### The Award

**Nicholas received:** **£448 sterling** - only **39% of his claim**

**Why so little?**
- Likely because he tried to claim **two enslaved men as "losses"**
- British commissioners probably rejected human property claims
- This reduced his compensation significantly

**But it was enough:**
- Provided means to return to family in Bahamas
- Funded his return to America
- Gave him capital for land speculation

## RETURN TO AMERICA: HOW DID HE REUNITE WITH FAMILY?

### The Journey Home (1786-1788)

**1786:** Nicholas returns to Bahamas with £448  
**Decision made:** Return to America (the nation he fought against)

### The Path Back

**c. 1788:** Nicholas and remaining children return to **United States**

**Why South Carolina first?**
1. **Family connections** - Brothers Thomas and William Welch in SC
2. **Land opportunities** - SC offering land grants to settlers
3. **Charleston as hub** - Best place for land speculation and reconnecting with scattered family
4. **Less animosity** - SC backcountry more forgiving than NC

### South Carolina (1788-1811)

**1790 Census:** Nicholas in **Orangeburg District, SC**
- Listed next to Tory Captain John Salley Sr.
- Beginning to re-establish himself

**1793:** Nicholas becomes **LAND SPECULATOR**
- **Six land grants** in South Carolina
- **1,000 acres each = 6,000 total acres**
- Locations:
  * Charleston District (1,000 acres)
  * Orangeburg District (2,000 acres)
  * Pendleton/96 District (3,000 acres)

**How did he afford this?**
- £448 from London (seed capital)
- **Surveying skills** - Nicholas learned "carrying the chains" as young man
- Knowledge of land markets and refugee title trading
- Working Charleston's legal mechanisms
- Possibly the mysterious British gold from war years

**1794:** Marries **Sarah Farren** in **Philadelphia** (age 56)
- **Mystery:** What was he doing in Philadelphia?
- Possibly traveling from SC
- Possibly land business
- Possibly meeting Sarah Farren for first time (or reunion?)
- **Was Sarah Farren a spy? Did they meet in London?** (speculative)

**1795:** Land dealings with brothers Thomas and William
- Settling father John Welch's estate
- Selling NC land to George Whitley

### Louisiana Interlude (1799-1811)

**1799:** Nicholas moves to **Rapides Parish, Louisiana** (age ~61)
- Spanish Louisiana at this time
- Land speculation continues

**1803:** Louisiana Purchase
- Spanish → French → **United States**
- Nicholas becomes American citizen (irony complete)

**1810 Census:** Still in Rapides Parish
- Age ~72, still active

### How Did He Reunite With His Children?

**The scattered children reconverged over two decades:**

**In the Bahamas (1784-1786):**
- Six children with Nicholas
- Two daughters marry and stay (Mary Misick, one other)

**In South Carolina (1788-1800):**
- Lewis Welch appears in SC records with Nicholas
- Son Nicholas Jr. born in Pendleton County, SC (1786)
- Brothers William and Thomas already in SC
- Family network re-establishing

**In Kentucky (c. 1800-1810):**
- Lewis Welch moves to Hopkins County, Kentucky
- Nicholas reportedly "met his brother in Kentucky"
- Helping Lewis get land grants

**In Tennessee (1810-1814):**
- Nicholas moves to **Giles County, Tennessee** (c. 1811)
- Lewis in Perry/Wayne County by 1820
- Multiple Welch families in Lawrence, Perry, Wayne, Hardin, Gibson counties
- **The reunification complete**
