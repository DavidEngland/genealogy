Ex James Welsh
Last Will and Testament
Paxton Township, Lancaster County, Pennsylvania
January 21, 1754
Editorial Note: The testator's name appears as "James Willsh" in the original document, but evidence suggests this may be John Welch Sr. The executor named is "John Wellsh," who is likely John Welch Jr. (1712–1775), father of Major Nicholas Welch (c. 1738–1814). Nicholas was approximately 16 years old when this will was written and would have been the testator's grandson.
In the Name of God Amen

I, James [John?] Welch of the Township of Paxton & County of Lancaster and Province of Pennsylvania, being very sick and weak of body, but of perfect mind and memory, and calling to mind the mortality of the body that it is appointed for all men once to die, and therefore I recommend my soul to God that gave it, and my body to the earth to be buried in a Christian and decent manner. And as for such worldly goods that God hath been pleased to bestow on me, I dispose of in the manner and form as followeth, viz:

First: I give and bequeath to my beloved wife Mary Welch all the household furniture, with one-third part of the cows and sheep, with one horse, and her maintenance out of the place during her widowhood or till the two youngest boys, viz. Robert and Joseph, come to age. And in case that she sees fit to alter her place of residence, she is to have one hundred pounds from my four youngest sons, viz. Thomas, James, Robert, and Joseph.
Secondly: I give and bequeath unto my son John forty pounds, to be paid in the year of our Lord 1759.
Thirdly: I give and bequeath to my son-in-law William Peterson ten pounds, to be paid in the year 1759.
Fourthly: I give and bequeath unto my son Thomas one hundred and fifty acres of land from the middle of the Great Swamp and backward to the east corner, and one horse named Buck.
Fifthly: I give and bequeath unto my son James one hundred acres of land from the middle of the Great Swamp & backwards, joining James McKnaught.
Sixthly: I give and bequeath to my two sons Robert and Joseph the remainder of the land next the river.
Seventhly: I give and bequeath to my daughter Jean one bay horse with a large blaze in his forehead, with a side saddle and bridle, with forty pounds of money to be paid in the year 1759.
Eighthly: I give and bequeath to my daughter Ezabel [Isabel] fifty pounds when [she] come[s] to age.
Ninthly: I give and bequeath to my daughter Mary fifty pounds when she come[s] to age.
N.B. That these four sons—Thomas, James, Robert, and Joseph—is to pay all debts and legacies according as each of them possesses of the land.

And I order and appoint my well-beloved wife Mary Welch and son John Welch my executors, revoking all other wills and executors whatsoever.

As witness my hand and seal this 21st day of January, 1754.

I order the servant man Michel McDaniel for the benefit of the plantation, and Thomas to be his master. And I order that the two youngest [sons] have their victuals [food] and clothes until they be fit to do for themselves.

James J. Welch L.S.
Witnesses:
Richard McClure
William Anderson

The 3rd day of May, 1754, before me the subscriber came Richard McClure and William Anderson, the witnesses to the within written will, and on their corporal [oath] did declare and said that they were present and saw and heard James [John?] Welch, the testator, sign, seal, publish, and declare the within writing as and for his last will and testament, and that at the doing thereof he was of sound and disposing mind, memory, and understanding, according to the best of their knowledge, observation, and belief.

Edw. Shippen, D. Master
