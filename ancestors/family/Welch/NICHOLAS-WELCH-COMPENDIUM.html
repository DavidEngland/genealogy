The early census and land records reveal six men of this name born before 1770, and four more born before 1799 in North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, Georgia and Alabama. Thanks to Marcia McClure, Mike Welch, Jerry Butler, Pat Welch, Mary Winham, BennieLou Altom, Dorothy Butler Welsh, Brian (Gene) Welch and others for their insights and research which I appreciate because the Welches are not my line and they are quite difficult. My tentative analysis of these men is as follows, keeping in mind that conflicts still exist and erroneous conclusions are my fault.

Nicholas Welch Sr. (#1) - "The Loyalist Major"

Birth: c. 1740, Lancaster County, Pennsylvania
Death: December 1814, Giles County, Tennessee
Father: John Welch (b. 1716, d. 1769 Tryon Co, NC)
Mother: Elizabeth Whitesides, Welsh or German Lutherans of Lancaster Co, PA
Grandfather: William Welsh (d. c. 1748)
⚔️ RANK: MAJOR - Hamilton's Regiment, Royal British Army of North Carolina (Loyalist)
Marriage #1: Elizabeth (Moore?) — daughter of Moses Moore of Lancaster Co, PA then Tryon Co, NC (c. 1740 - c. 1780-1782)
Marriage #2: Sarah (b. 1751 VA, d. after 1850 Giles Co, TN)

The Epic Journey: Pennsylvania to Louisiana to Tennessee

6 Feb 1738 - John Welsh marries Elizabeth Whitesides by Rev. John Casper Stoever in Lancaster Co, PA (she died before 1742). These events support birth date of 1716 for John Welch and 1740 for Nicholas Welch.

24 Oct 1759 - John Welch granted land on waters of Catawba River, Anson Co, NC (later Mecklenburg, then Tryon, then Lincoln Counties)

1761 - John Welsh filed survey for this land; Nicholas Welsh was one of the chainbearers

12 July 1766 - William Welch of Craven Co, SC sold to Nicholas Welch of NC, 170 acres on Howards or Haywards Creek, Mecklenburg Co, NC

5 Jun 1769 - Will of John Welsh of Tryon Co, NC names sons Thomas and Nicholas Welsh as executors

20 Mar 1772 - Nicholas Welch granted 300 acres on waters of Indian Creek in Tryon Co, NC
["The History of Lincoln County, NC" locates the Tory Major Nicholas Welch on Indian Creek, close to Tory Lt. Col. John Moore, son of Capt. Moses Moore]

Revolutionary War Service - The Loyalist Cause

10 Mar to 12 Apr 1779 - Brothers Nicholas Welch, William Welch, and Thomas Welch serve 34 days in jail as POWs (Loyalists) in Greenwood, SC (96 District)
(William and Thomas later switched sides and took the oath. Nicholas remained a Loyalist.)

May 1781 - Nicholas Welch resigns British officer commission, flees to Savannah, GA to join family (wife and 9 children went there in late 1780)

Exile: From Florida to the Bahamas to London

1781-1783 - Moves to Florida with wife & 6 children (three are grown)
St. Johns Town / Hester's Bluff: Nicholas Welch documented at St. Johns Town, East Florida with property including house, warehouse, 9 slaves, and 500-acre land grant from Governor Patrick Tonyn
Florida History Online Reference

1783-1786 - Moves to New Providence, Bahamas, where two of his daughters marry

1763 - David, John and William go to Craven County, South Carolina, and settle on both sides of Lynches Creek

Revolutionary War Context

Historical Note: c. 1778 John Welsh of Camden District one of the petitioners to establish the Upper Fork of Lynches Creek Baptist Church.

(See the Mel Gibson movie "The Patriot" to get a flavor of this area and the pressure on the locals to support the British at first then switch sides to the Patriots. Mel Gibson's character Benjamin Martin is based on Francis Marion and William Washington, the son of Baptist Elder William Washington who preached at the Lower Fork of Lynches Creek Baptist Church. Col. William Washington led the Patriot militia at the Battle of Cowpens on January 17, 1781, the climactic battle depicted in the movie. William Washington Welch was likely a namesake.)

1787 - John Welch, and his son Nicholas Welch file claims for losses sustained in SC during Revolution
(claims denied — John Welch served briefly with British)

1787 - David Welch, and his son William Welch file claims for losses sustained in SC during Revolution
(claims denied — David Welch served briefly with British)

1787 - William Welch, brother of the above David and John Welch, files claim for losses sustained in SC during Revolution
(claim APPROVED because he was a Patriot) — the Tory William Welch was his uncle from Lincoln Co, NC, who moved to Pendleton District, SC, then Wayne Co, TN

Alabama Settlement

Also among the early settlers of Perry Co were John Jacob Wolfe (1820) and John Pitts (1820) whose brother Mark Pitts married Susannah Hembree in Pendleton District, SC.

About This Compendium

This document organizes and modernizes genealogical research originally compiled by Larry Petrisky and published on Genealogy.com forums. The research distinguishes between eleven different men named Nicholas Welch who lived between 1725-1860 across Pennsylvania, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, Georgia, Alabama, Louisiana, Missouri, and Arkansas.
