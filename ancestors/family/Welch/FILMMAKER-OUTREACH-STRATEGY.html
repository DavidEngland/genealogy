PITCH PACKAGE COMPONENTS

The One-Pager

THE LOYALIST MAJOR
The Extraordinary Life of Nicholas Welch

Logline:
Major Nicholas Welch chose loyalty to the King over American independence. It cost him everything: his land, his wife, his freedom. Exiled to the Bahamas, he petitioned the Crown in London and received just £448—barely enough to make one last gamble: return to the United States as an enemy and rebuild.

Genre: Historical Drama / Documentary
Setting: 1738-1814, Pennsylvania to Tennessee via Florida, Bahamas, and London
Themes: Loyalty vs. survival, reconciliation, American reinvention, the Revolution as civil war

Why Now:

  • Polarization: Americans need stories of reconciliation after division
  • Untold history: Loyalist experience rarely depicted
  • Primary sources: Nicholas left his own words (1786 Memorial)
  • Locations: Battle sites, archives, and landscapes still accessible
  • Descendants: Living family members, DNA research ongoing

Comparable Films:

  • 12 Years a Slave (journey through suffering, primary source basis)
  • The Revenant (survival and endurance)
  • Master and Commander (British military perspective in 18th century)
  • Ken Burns's The Civil War (if documentary - primary sources, letters, landscapes)

Format Options:

  • Feature film: 2 hours, theatrical release
  • Limited series: 6-8 episodes, prestige streaming
  • Documentary: 2 hours or 3-part series, PBS/streaming

Attachments:

  • Book proposal (35 chapters outlined)
  • Primary source: 1786 Memorial (full text)
  • Timeline: Complete 1738-1814 chronology
  • Locations: Research guide to all sites
  • Family tree: 16+ children, living descendants
  • Consultation: Available historians, WikiTree community

Contact: [Your information]

The Treatment (3-5 Pages)

Include:

Page 1: Setup

  • Nicholas's birth in Pennsylvania (German immigrant family)
  • Move to North Carolina, marriage to Elizabeth Moore
  • Accumulated 600+ acres by 1775
  • Revolutionary tensions begin

Page 2: Conflict

  • Chose King over independence (why?)
  • Twice arrested for treason
  • Recruited 270 Loyalists
  • Appointed Major, Hamilton's Regiment
  • Battle of Ramsour's Mill (June 20, 1780) - THE TURNING POINT
  • Three sons captured
  • Family banished

Page 3: Exile

  • Elizabeth dies during exodus (when? where? how?)
  • St. Johns Town, East Florida (1782-1784)
  • Spain takes Florida (1783)—must flee again
  • Bahamas refuge (1784-1786)
  • Desperation decision: Go to London

Page 4: Petition

  • March 20, 1786: Memorial filed
  • THE GAME-CHANGER: FIVE British officers testify for him
    • LORD CORNWALLIS (Commander-in-Chief!)
    • LORD RAWDON (Second-in-command)
    • LT. COL. HAMILTON (his direct CO)
    • Two other British officers
  • This is extraordinary testimony - most had 1-2 witnesses
  • The Commander who surrendered at Yorktown vouching for him
  • Detailed claim of losses: £1,150
  • Months of waiting in London
  • Award: £448 (only 39%)
  • Despite Cornwallis's support, still low
  • But enough to make one last gamble
  • The impossible decision: Return to America

Page 5: Redemption

  • c.1788: Back in South Carolina
  • 1793: 6,000 acres acquired—HOW?
  • 1794: Married Sarah Farren (Philadelphia)
  • Louisiana period
  • Tennessee pioneer
  • Death 1814
  • Sarah lives to 99 (1851)

THE POINT:
This is not a story about the American Revolution. It's a story about what happens after you lose, after you bet on the wrong side, after everything is taken from you. It's about resilience, reinvention, and the surprisingly American idea that you can start over.

RED FLAGS TO AVOID

Don't:

  • Pitch Nicholas as a "hero" (he owned slaves, fought against American independence)
  • Ignore the complexity (if you make him too sympathetic, you lose credibility)
  • Present as "Southern Lost Cause" narrative (this is not about glorifying the losing side)
  • Claim it's "the untold story" (other Loyalists have been documented, just not as completely)

Do:

  • Present as morally complex
  • Emphasize the JOURNEY and RESILIENCE, not the cause he fought for
  • Frame as "American Revolution as civil war" (neighbors vs. neighbors)
  • Highlight the RECONCILIATION aspect (he returned, lived peacefully, became American)
  • Acknowledge he owned slaves (and what happened to them is a question worth exploring)

ETHNIC ORIGIN ANGLE

Re: User's note "Thought was Welsh, but perhaps could be Scot-Irish or German"

Use this as a THEME:

"Nicholas Welch didn't fit neat categories. Was he Welsh? German? His parents married in a German Reformed church in Pennsylvania. He settled among Scots-Irish in Carolina. He fought for the British Empire. He returned to America. His identity was fluid, contested, complicated—like America itself."

This makes the story MORE interesting:

  • Pennsylvania German community → Carolina Scots-Irish settlement → British Army → American citizen
  • Cultural bridge: Could recruit in multiple communities
  • Immigration story: What does it mean to be "American" when America is just being invented?
  • Cornwallis found Moore & Welch "favourable" because they could navigate multiple ethnic/linguistic communities

Documentary angle: Explore Pennsylvania German identity, Scots-Irish settlement, British military culture, American frontier identity—four cultural worlds Nicholas moved through.
