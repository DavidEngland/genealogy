#### Preliminary Search Results:

Records of Commissions and Committees, etc
1763-1789 East Florida Claims Commission: Papers and Reports
Claims and reports of the East Florida Claims Commission and lists of title deeds and papers relating to the management of estates. Consists primarily of the claims, with supporting documents, of the settlers of the province of East Florida which in 1783, after twenty years of British administration, was ceded to the Crown of Spain under article 5 of the Treaty of Paris.
The series contains:
Files 1-18 are made up by the claimants' papers, usually in the form of a memorial supported by documentation of land grants, indentures, schedules and valuations of property, and often with plats and other maps and plans.
File 19 consists of reports on the claims of the many settlers who had taken refuge in the Bahamas.
File 20 contains papers relating to the administration of East Florida, in particular with regard to events consequent upon the cession
Files 21-30 are largely fragmentary and relate principally to administrative matters, although they do include as well some documentation, including land grants and plats, that has become separated from the main series of claimants' papers (files 1-18): these are not indexed.

## GENETIC/ETHNIC ORIGIN RESEARCH

### The "Welsh vs. Scot-Irish vs. German" Question

**Evidence for GERMAN origin (Pennsylvania German):**
- Parents married by **Rev. Johann Casper Stoever** (German Reformed minister)
- Lancaster County, PA (heavily German settlement)
- Father: **John Welch** (Anglicized from "Johann Welsch"?)
- Mother: **Elizabeth Whitesides** (could be German "Weisseite")
- Nicholas's will witnesses: many German surnames

**Evidence for WELSH origin:**
- Surname: "Welch" often = "Welsh"  
- "William" common Welsh given name (Nicholas's father and sons)
- Settlement pattern: Welsh Tract in PA, Welsh migration to Carolinas

**Evidence for SCOTS-IRISH origin:**
- Migration path: PA → Great Wagon Road → Carolina backcountry (classic Scots-Irish)
- Presbyterian connections (many Scots-Irish were Presbyterian)
- Neighborhood in NC: Many Scots-Irish surnames (Moore, Graham, etc.)

### Leading Theory: **GERMAN-WELSH MIX**

**Hypothesis:**
- Father John Welch was German ("Welsch" = German word for foreign/Romance-speaking)
- OR father was Welsh who lived in German community
- Nicholas grew up in German-speaking Lancaster County
- Marriage to Elizabeth Moore tied him to Scots-Irish network
- Cultural identity: "Pennsylvania German" by upbringing, "British subject" by loyalty

### Why Cornwallis Found Moore & Welch "Favourable"

**Lieutenant Colonel John Moore** (Nicholas's father-in-law):
- Family from Ulster, Ireland (Scots-Irish)
- Successful landowner, respected in community
- King's representative = local authority figure
- When Moore declared for King, many neighbors followed

**Nicholas Welch:**
- Son-in-law of Lt. Col. Moore
- Established landowner (600+ acres)
- Literate, educated (appointed executor of father's estate age ~29)
- Could recruit in German dialect among PA German settlers in Carolina
- Multi-cultural bridge: German heritage, Scots-Irish connections, English language

**British Strategy:**
Cornwallis needed local leaders who could:
1. Recruit in multiple languages/communities
2. Had local credibility and land holdings
3. Had extended family networks

Nicholas Welch fit perfectly:
- German dialect for PA German immigrants
- English for Scottish/English settlers  
- Family connections through Moore
- Property owner (financial stake in British victory)

### DNA Evidence Strategy
**To determine ethnic origin:**
- Y-DNA test (paternal line): Would show Welch/Welsh paternal haplogroup
- Autosomal DNA: Would show British Isles vs. Germanic percentages
- Surname projects: "Welch DNA Project" on FamilyTreeDNA
- Compare with known German-American and Welsh-American lines
