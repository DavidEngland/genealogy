EXCURSIS ON ZACHARIAH WELCH

Here arises some question/debate as to whether Zacariah Welch of Anson County, North Carolina might actually be the son of Henry and Sarah Welch, of Chatham County, North Carolina; rather than of William Welchennelt, born in Ireland, occupation hatter; and his wife Mary (Queens) Welch, born in Scotland.
Entries found on Genforum and Ancestry.com-message board, both state that the Zachariah Welch whoresided in Anson County, was born in Dublin, Ireland in 1772, and came to America with his parents when he was about six months old.However these statements made in print appear to be unproven; or at the least, have not been well documented by factual information that I have been able to find.The apparent principle source for this statement pertaining to his place of birth comes from, “The Tishomingo County Connection” History of Skinner-Welch * Adams- Seago and Allied Familes of Tishomingo County,
Mississippi, which was compiled by Esther Welch Adams of River Heights, Crump, Tennesseee 38327, (1955 - 1980), of which a second printing was made in 1981.It's call #929.273 - Ad 25, is to be found in the Tishomingo County Archives, located in the old historic courthouse, in the count seat at Wadesboro.
According to Mrs. Adams manuscript, on page 269, she writes that he, meaning William Welch “was said to have been a Welchenvelt, in Ireland”.Notice that it “was said” and thus not neccesarily proved by sound documentation.There is no citationsin her manuscript pertaining to a ships passenger list, extant bible records for births and marriages or verifable deeds, proven wills, etc. to substantiate that William Welch/Welchenvelt was married to Mary Queens, that they immigrated from Dublin, Ireland, in 1772, and that “their ninth child Zachariah...” was born prior to the families migration to the Colonies or on board a ship, or that William and Mary Welch ever ventured into Anson County, North Carolina.This story may have been taken merely from family tradition, or strickly adapted from stories/legends handed down over generations; or possibly by conjecture.
Mrs. Adams does list William and Mary (Queens) Welch's children on page 269, as:
JEREMIAH WELCH, who died sometime after September 1, 1780; MARY Welch, born circa 1750, who remained unmarried; Augustus Welch, born circa 1752, who was killed in the Indian Wars; ROSEANNAH Welch, born circa 1754, who married Richard Russell; ELIZABETH Welch, who married William Benton, CATHERINE Welch, who married Henry Hunter; SARAH Welch, who married a Harris; NANCY Welch, who married a Laurence and ZACHARIAH WELCH, who married Priscilla Powell Carter.
What makes this list so intriging are the similarities in the christian names associated with William and Mary (Queens) Welch list of children and the children described in a partition deed, presumably for the heirs of Henry Welch Sr. that was filed in Chatham County Deed Book “N”, page 229 on April 2, 1803.This deed was executed between Lemuel Smith of the first part, and: Elijah Hunter and CATHERINE his wife, Elizabeth Welch, William Benton and MARY his wife, Henry Welch Jr., EDMOND Welch, ROSANNAH Welch, SARAH Welch, ZACHARIAH Welch, JEREMIAHWelch, and NANCY Welch.All of the above were identified as the, “heirs at law and legatees of Henry Welch Sr., deceased, and Sarah, his widow.”The tract in question was situated on Tick Creek, being a part of the Survey of Enoch Lewis, and adjacent to the lands belonging to Luke Welch.Notice closley the similarities between the names of the children in these two families:

William and Mary Welch, by the list of Mrs. Adams, have nine children; whileHenry and Sarah Welch, by the Chatham County deed, have a total of ten children, most all of them match by name, to wit: Zachariah, Jeremiah, Roseannah/Rosannah, Sarah, Nancy and Catherine, Elizabeth and Mary.A total of eight children from each household having the same given names.Three of them, in each household, have unordinary names such as: Zachariah, Jeremiah and Roseannah/Rosannah Welch, which are not as typical a surname as John, James, William, Thomas or Anna, Margaret or Martha; although Mary, Sarah, Nancy and Elizabeth are names used quite commonly during the period.Only the name of Augustus Welch is inconsistent; and since his noteddeath is reported during the, “in Indian Wars”, his very existance remains unprovable.
Now for some of the differences.In Mrs. Adams records she states that Mary Welch, presumably died unmarried, and Elizabeth Welch married William Benton; whereas Henry and Sarah's daughter Mary is specifically recorded in the deed book as having married to William Benton.Could Mrs. Adams perhaps have crossed these two?
This William Benton, husband of Mary, left his will probated at Wadesborough on July 19, 1820, naming his sons: JEREMIAH and William HENRY Benton, and daughters: ROSANNA Moore, Penelope White, Margaret HUNTER Allen and SARAH HARRIS Benton.JEREMIAH BENTON acted as one of the executors.Here again, in a disportionate number, are the same christian names implied again. The will abstracts are contained in the Anson County Archives, which is located in the block behind the deed recorder's office.This will abstract has penciled in this William Benton'swife's name of Mary and the names to the spouses of each of his married daughters: Dickerson Moore, Joseph White and William Allen.The biograpies of William Benton's daughters and who they married are also contained in manuscripts contained in the Wadesboro Public Library. Notice here the names of three of his daughters: ROSANNA Moore, Margaret HUNTER Allen and SARAH HARRIS Benton, which names are here being capitalized, are the same as Zachariah's sister Sarah who married Harris, and his daughter Sarah Harris Welch.Rosanna Moore matches, Zachariah's sister and his daughter and also the daughter's name for Henry and Sarah Welch.While JEREMIAH matches as well in all three listings as being Zarchariah's sibling, his son and the son of Henry and Sarah Welch.
The tombstone for Zachariah Welch list his years of birth and death and that he was born in Dublin, Ireland.Mrs. Adams writes on page 272B, “There were no monuments to any of these (meaning the graves of Zachariah, Priscilla and their daughter Elizabeth Ann) until about 1970 when relatives had them placed.” So that the text,”Born in Dublin, Ireland” which appears on his headstone, was not inscribed on an early tombstone, with this inscription then being transferred onto a newer replacement marker.So do the exact dates for Zachariah's birth and death entered in Mrs. Adams manuscript, but not on the tombstones; and his place of birth, Dublin, Ireland, come from an unpublished Bible record; or are they held to by oral tradition?
On September 1, 1791, John May Jr. conveyed to Henry Welch, “of the county of Chatham” for the sum of 250 pounds, a tract of 200 acres situated near Gould's Fork in Anson County. On January 10, 1799, Joshua Prout conveyed to Henry Welch a lot lying in the town of Wadesborough, beginning at a stake in Rutherford Street and runs south wt. (with) Rutherford Street nine poles to Morgan Street...containing half an acre.” known as No. 36.Mrs. Adams then comments on page 270. “Aug. 15, 1823, Zachariah Welch sold land to James Slaughter at Wadesboro, Book U, page 362”.She makes no description of the tract or lot of land being conveyed. On August 15th 1823, Zachariah Welch sold this same town lot No. 36, that was purchased by Henry Welch in 1799, to James Slaughter.This seems odd since (1) the only Henry Welch in Anson County, is clearly identified as being from Chatham County, and (2) there is no known connection/association between Zachariah Welch of Anson County, or with any member of his Welch connections to any Henry Welch.Zachariah, of Anson County, reportedly had no siblings names Henry, and his parents are again listed as William and Mary, and not as Henry and Sarah.Unfortuneately, there is no deed or will in Anson County signifying as to how Zachariah came into possession of this town lot.
On April 22, 1805, Henry and his brother Jeremiah Welch, both witnessed a deed executed between John White and William Benton, both of Anson County asrecorded in (ACDB “L”, page 1). Note that one of William Benton's daughter's married a Joseph White.The White genealogy at the Wadesboro Public Library, traces Joseph White's lineageto Moses White, from Old Swede's Church, in New Castle County Delaware, which is where the Welch's of Chatham County originated, before moving into North Carolina.
By 1796, John Welch of Chatham County, North Carolina had moved to Anson County, North Carolina.A deed recorded in Anson County on October 15th of that year
from Patrick Boggan to John Welch, conveyed to said Welch a tract of 100 acres on the waters of Gould's Fork, of Brown Creek, beginning at a stake the second corner of said Boggans 160 acres tract in Aaron Bakers second line.This exact same tract was sold by William R. Pickett, Sheriff of Anson County, on September 14, 1807, to John May, presumably for unpaid debts, against the heirs of John Welch, deceased to wit: Polly, Patsey, Luke, John, Jane, Sally and Edmund Welch.
Henry and Sarah Welch, of Chatham County also had a son named Edmund/Edmond Welch.So both John and his uncle Henry Welch, who were both natives of Chatham County, both had descendants who settled in Anson County, and both had sons named Edmund.
On the 1800 Anson County census, the household of John and his nephew Henry, who was the probable son of Henry and Sarah Welch, appear on the Anson County census residing in Fayettville District, page 223A.The only other Welch then residing in Anson County was Zachariah, as a single man, on page 214 A, also living in Fayettville District.
Zachariah, allegedly the son of William and Mary, named one of his sons Edmund or Edmond Lilly Welch.Mrs. Adams does include a story in her manuscript, on page 270, which shows as to how Zachariah came about naming his son Edmund/Edmond Lilly Welch.Edmund Lilly was apparently named for a brother of a girl whom Priscilla knew,
who had wanted to marry Priscilla.In, “The Annals of the Allens” by Mary Allen Huntley, published August 29, 1954, found in the Wadesborough - Hampton B. Allen Library, 120 S. Greene St., contains, beginning on page 4, an account of an Edmnd Lilly, formerly of Fluvana County, Virginia and Halifax County, North Carolina, who settled on the Pee Dee River in Anson County.The author writes, “He was born in 1728, and was married three times.... He was an old man nearing his eightieth birthday when his last child was born...”While this Edmund would certainly be too old for prospects of marrying Priscilla, he could have been the father or grandfather of another Edmund Lilly, who could have been Priscilla's secret admirer.Further research on this surname is warranted to prove out this possibility.
Zachariah Welch, settled on Cribbs Creek, in Anson County; while John Welch settled on Gould's Fork.Both Cribbs Creek and Gould's Fork are situated just west of Wadesborough.However, Zachariah and John could have lived as far as ten to fifteen miles apart.
A William Welchich does appear in early Anson County records, as a witness to several documents, but never as a land owner.One of these deeds dated August 5, 1761,
is a deed of conveyance for 100 acres from Richard Yarbrough to Shadrack Jacobs.Both William Welshear and James Welsh appear on the 1763, Anson County tax list.
Mrs. Adams writes on page 269, “William may have visited here in January of 1756 and bought acreage on the Catawba River, since deeds made on January 21, and 22 of that year were recorded.Robert Welch recorded land on the Catawba River in October 1756.”It would be highly improbable for William the father of Zachariah Welch, to have acquired lands in Anson County in 1756, then returned to Dublin, Ireland for sixteen years, and then return to the American colonies. The two deeds dated January 21st. and 22nd. are actually one.The 22nd is a continuance of the deed of conveyance from Avington and Shana Sherrill to William Wilsh, for land lying on Catawba River.There are no deeds for the dispositon of these lands, nor is there any indication that Zachariah had any connection to this land or to this William Wilsh.
A host of Welch's descending from both Luke and Henry Welch also lived in Anson County at this time.This Zachariah Welch of Anson County had two sons named Edmund Lily and Jeremiah Augustus White Welch, which names (meaning Edmund and Jeremiah) are found among Henry and Sarah's children.These surnames seldom are identified elsewhere outside of this Welch family located in the south.Out of all of this, of Zachariah's four sons, none are named William for his father, nor is there a daughter named Mary, for his mother; but he did name one of his daughters Sarah, possibly for his mother, if she was indeed Sarah and not Mary, and for two of his sisters namely Nancy and Rosanna. Again Mrs. Adams made reference to Rosanna Welch, Zarahariah's sister as having married a Richard Russell.In both Chatham County deeds recorded in deed book “N”, pages 229 and 231, which pertain to the sale of Henry Welch's property in Chatham County by his wife and his children and heirs, one of the witnesses to both deeds is recorded as being Richard Russell; and also a Wm. Pickett.A William R. Pickett
was serving as sheriff of Anson County, when forced sale of John Welch's land's occurred.
There are no records for William and Mary (Queens) Welch in Anson County.Neither are there any records of Zachariah's siblings, as listed by Mrs. Adams, identified as residing there; save possibly for Rosanna Russell and Mrs. Elizabth/Mary Benton.Both the Russell's and the Benton's can equally be associated with the Chatham County Welch's.
However, there is no indication by existing records that any of the Chatham County Welch's were hatters.Henry Welch had three brothers, Michael, Luke and Walter.Three brothers all left wills identifying their heirs and property.Henry did not.Only the deed mentioned earlier serves to identify his children.It is doubtful, at this time, to believe that Henry Welch was associated with hat making.
In April 2011, I made a two week trip to Tishomingo County, Mississippi; Anson County, North Carolina; and to Wayne and Perry Counties, Tennessee.I copied all of the Welch deeds and their varants from the beginning of Anson county until the mid 1820's when all deeds pertaining to Welch's ceased until the early 1870's.Zachariah left no record of his apparent brief stay in Wayne County.All Perry County records, prior to 1840, have been lost due to early courthouse fires.While I am mow 75% certain that this Zachariah Welch of Anson County, is the indeed son of Henry and Sarah. However there yet remains some troublesome doubt, such as: Catherine/Mary Welch having married Elijah/Henry Hunter. and Elizabeth/Mary Welch having married William Benton.Also with the Anson County Welch's having been “hatters” and Mrs. Adams many unnotedsources; this issue will remain unresolved until further documented research is done, from primary sources establishing this Zarchariah's true identity.
