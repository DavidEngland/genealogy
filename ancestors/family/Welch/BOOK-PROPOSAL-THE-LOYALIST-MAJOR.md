## ELEVATOR PITCH (30 seconds)

Major Nicholas Welch chose the losing side in the American Revolution—and it cost him everything. Imprisoned, banished, his family stripped bare, he fled to Florida, then the Bahamas, then London to beg the King for compensation. Against all odds, he returned to America—the very nation he'd fought against—and rebuilt his life on the Tennessee frontier. This is the untold story of a Loyalist's journey from ruin to redemption, and a powerful reminder that American history has many sides.

---

## HOOK (First paragraph of book)

*On June 20, 1780, Major Nicholas Welch stood on a field near Ramsour's Mill in North Carolina, resplendent in his brilliant red British uniform, a fortune in gold coins jingling in his pockets. Around him, 1,300 Loyalist militiamen—men he had personally recruited with oratory and guineas—waited for battle. Across the field, 400 Patriot militia prepared to attack. By day's end, three of Nicholas's sons would be prisoners, his military career would be over, and his family would face banishment and ruin. But his story was far from finished. The worst—and the most remarkable—chapters lay ahead.*

## MARKET ANALYSIS

### Comparable Titles

1. **"The Loyalist: A Novel" by James Casper (2020)**
   - Fiction about Loyalist experience
   - Well-reviewed but fictional

2. **"Liberty's Exiles: American Loyalists in the Revolutionary World" by Maya Jasanoff (2011)**
   - Academic, broad survey of Loyalist diaspora
   - Pulitzer Prize finalist
   - **Our book:** More personal, single narrative, accessible

3. **"Tories: Fighting for the King in America's First Civil War" by Thomas B. Allen (2010)**
   - Survey of multiple Loyalists
   - **Our book:** Deep dive, one family, more emotional

4. **"The Men Who Lost America: British Leadership, the American Revolution, and the Fate of the Empire" by Andrew Jackson O'Shaughnessy (2013)**
   - British perspective, leadership focus
   - **Our book:** Ground-level, personal story

5. **"American Loyalists: Transcript of Various Records" (genealogy)**
   - Dry, documentary
   - **Our book:** Narrative, compelling story

### What Makes This Book Unique

1. **Single, Complete Narrative:** Follows one man birth to death
2. **Geographic Breadth:** PA to NC to FL to Bahamas to London to SC to LA to TN
3. **Primary Sources:** Nicholas's own 1786 Memorial, claims testimony
4. **Redemption Arc:** Not just defeat, but return and reinvention
5. **Family Story:** 16 children scattered across America
6. **Accessible History:** Academic rigor, narrative appeal
