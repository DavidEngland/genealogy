# Major Nicholas Welch: A Complete Timeline (c.1738-1814)

## The Epic Journey of a Loyalist Major: From Pennsylvania to Tennessee via Florida, the Bahamas, and London

---

## PART I: PENNSYLVANIA ROOTS (1716-1760)

### **1716**
**Father John Welch born** in Lancaster County, Pennsylvania

### **6 February 1738**
**Parents marry:** John Welsh marries Elizabeth Whitesides by Rev. John Casper Stoever in Lancaster Co, PA  
*Elizabeth died before 1742*

### **c.1738-1740**
**NICHOLAS WELCH BORN** in Lancaster County, Pennsylvania  
*Son of John Welch and Elizabeth Whitesides*

### **c.1742**
**Mother dies:** Nicholas is approximately 2-4 years old when his mother Elizabeth Whitesides dies

### **c.1748**
**Grandfather William Welsh dies**

### **15 Dec 1739**
**Uncle's marriage:** Jacob Wolfe Welsch marries Elizabeth —— by Rev. John Casper Stoever in Lancaster Co, PA  
*Jacob's first son named John Jacob Welsh. Jacob had brother William Welsh whose first son was John Peter Welsh*

### **10 May 1748**
**Moore family connection:** Moses Moor marries (2nd) ------- Gillighin by Rev. John Casper Stoever in Lancaster Co, PA  
*Moses Moore is father of Lt. Col John Moore of Lincoln Co, NC. The Moores were Baptists, soldiers, gunsmiths/blacksmiths in NC*

### **1750s**
**Nicholas grows up** in Lancaster County, Pennsylvania German Lutheran community  
*Scotch-Irish Presbyterian immigration transforming the Appalachian frontier*

## PART II: MIGRATION TO CAROLINA (1759-1772)

### **24 October 1759**
**Father's land grant:** John Welch granted land on waters of Catawba River in Anson Co, NC  
*What became Mecklenburg, then Tryon, then Lincoln Counties*  
**Nicholas (age ~19-21) is involved in family migration southward**

### **1761**
**Nicholas as chainbearer:** John Welsh filed survey for Catawba River land  
**NICHOLAS WELSH was one of the chainbearers**  
*First documented appearance of Nicholas in Carolina*

### **11 February 1762**
**Cousin William's land:** William Welch (b.c.1734 VA, d.1818 SC), cousin of Nicholas Welch Sr, granted 150 acres on Lynches Creek (River) in Craven Co (later Cheraws District), SC  
*Lynches R. divided Camden & Cheraws Districts. William remained on this land until 1797*

### **c.1757-1762**
**MARRIAGE #1 (possibly):** Unknown first wife mentioned by genealogist Connie Voss  
*Identity and dates unknown*

### **c.1757-1762**
**MARRIAGE #2:** Nicholas Welch marries **ELIZABETH E. MOORE** (c.1740 - c.1780-1782)  
*Daughter traditionally identified as daughter of John William Moore of New Kent and Hanover Counties, VA*  
*Alternative theory: daughter of Moses Moore of Lancaster Co, PA*  
*No definitive primary source for parentage*

### **12 July 1766**
**Nicholas purchases land:** William Welch, of Craven Co, SC, sold to Nicholas Welch of NC, 170 acres on Howards or Haywards Creek, Mecklenburg Co, NC  
**Nicholas (age ~26-28) is now a landowner**

### **23 January 1767**
**Brother Thomas's land:** Thomas Welch, brother of Nicholas Welch Sr, granted 300 acres on Howard or Hayward Creek in Mecklenburg Co, NC

### **5 June 1769**
**Father's death:** Will of John Welsh of Tryon Co, NC names sons **Thomas and Nicholas Welsh as executors**  
**Nicholas (age ~29-31) administers father's estate**

### **20 March 1772**
**Major land grant:** Nicholas Welch granted **300 acres on waters of Indian Creek** in Tryon Co, NC  
*"The History of Lincoln County, NC" locates the Tory Major Nicholas Welch on Indian Creek, close to Tory Lt. Col. John Moore, son of Capt. Moses Moore*  
**Nicholas is now a substantial landowner with 470+ acres**

### **1772-1775**
**Prosperity and family:** Nicholas established as "man of considerable substance"  
*Through land, marriage, and family, deeply rooted in Carolina soil*  
*By this time has multiple children: John, William C., Judith, Nicholas William, Mary, Margaret E., Thomas (1st), Sarah, Ann, Hester (1st)*

## PART III: REVOLUTION AND PERSECUTION (1775-1779)

### **1775-1776**
**Allegiance chosen:** From "onset of the dissentions in America," Nicholas's sympathies lay with the King  
*This allegiance made him a target for ascendant Patriot authorities*

### **1776-1777**
**Twice arrested:** Nicholas arrested and imprisoned twice on charges of **"Treason against the State"**  
*Secured release only through intervention of attorneys*  
*Cost: "One hundred & fifty pounds" - a significant sum*

### **1777-1778**
**Impossible situation:** Finding it impossible to remain in North Carolina without taking up arms against the Crown

### **1778**
**Raises a regiment:** Hearing of expedition against Georgia, Nicholas assembles force of **270 local Loyalists** and "sat out for that Province"  
*Leaves family and property behind*

### **Late 1778 - Early 1779**
**Brutal march:** Welch's column pursued and attacked **three times** by Rebel forces  
*Over 50 men killed, wounded, or taken prisoner*  
*Others abandoned the enterprise*

### **February 1779**
**Briar Creek, Georgia:** Nicholas and determined remnant of **90 men** join Colonel Archibald Campbell's army at Briar Creek, Georgia  
*Leadership and commitment immediately recognized*

### **February 1779**
**Commission received:** Appointed **MAJOR of the Royal No. Carolina Regiment** (Hamilton's Regiment)  
*Nicholas (age ~39-41) is now a British officer*

### **10 March to 12 April 1779**
**Prisoner of War:** Brothers Nicholas Welch, William Welch, and Thomas Welch serve **34 days in jail as POWs (Loyalists)** in Greenwood, SC (96 District)  
*William and Thomas later switch sides and take oath*  
*Nicholas remains a Loyalist*

### **1779-1780**
**Recruiting service:** Frequently ventured "privately into the enemy's Country" to recruit other Loyalists  
*Successfully enlisted considerable number of men for His Majesty's Service*

---

## PART IV: RAMSOUR'S MILL AND CATASTROPHE (1780)

### **Spring 1780**
**Charleston falls:** British capture Charleston, SC (May 12, 1780)  
*Lord Cornwallis's Southern Strategy: timely uprising of organized local Loyalists supported by British regulars*

### **May-June 1780**
**The brilliant red uniform:** Nicholas appears in community dressed in "brilliant red uniform"  
*Exhibiting "a considerable quantity of gold coins"*  
*Representing himself as Major of Hamilton's Regiment*  
*Great orator using "judicious use of his guineas" to rally supporters*

**Contemporary account:**
> "Nicholas Welch, who had lived just above Moore on Indian Creek, went from this vicinity eighteen months prior to this and joined the British army. He appeared dressed in a new uniform and exhibiting a considerable quantity of gold coins, representing himself as Major of Hamilton's Regiment. He urged the men to embody at once, telling of the fall of Charleston, Buford's defeat, and the bad condition of affairs for the Americans everywhere. By his narratives and judicious use of his guineas he prevailed over Moore and it was determined to organize at once."

### **Mid-June 1780**
**Loyalist gathering:** Approximately **1,300 Loyalists** gather at Ramsour's Mill  
*Nicholas and Lt. Colonel John Moore chose to act prematurely*  
*Lord Cornwallis had expressly advised NC Loyalists to remain quiet and wait for support of main British army*

### **20 June 1780**
**BATTLE OF RAMSOUR'S MILL - DISASTER**  
*Location: Lincolnton, North Carolina*

**Forces:**
- Loyalists: ~1,300 men (Nicholas Welch, Lt. Col. John Moore)
- Patriots: ~400 militia (Colonel Francis Locke)

**Battle details:**
- Smaller Patriot force launches surprise attack
- Chaotic, brutal affair fought between neighbors and kinsmen
- No uniforms to distinguish friend from foe
  - Patriots pinned white paper to hats
  - Loyalists wore green twigs
- **Smaller Patriot force routed larger, less-organized Loyalist militia**

**Consequences:**
- **Three of Nicholas's sons captured:** Thomas, William, and Nicholas Jr.
- Devastating blow to Loyalist cause
- Shattered Cornwallis's recruitment strategy
- British commanders furious

### **June-October 1780**
**Nicholas continues service:** Remains with British forces despite family catastrophe

### **7 October 1780**
**Battle of King's Mountain:** Decisive Patriot victory  
*Further disaster for Loyalist cause*

### **Late 1780**
**Family banished:** Following King's Mountain, North Carolina authorities move swiftly against families of prominent Loyalists  
**Wife Elizabeth and 9 children formally "Banished that Province"**

**Confiscation:**
- All property seized
- **600 acres of developed land**
- **Two valuable Negro Slaves**
- Family "stript... of every thing they had, except only a small part of their wearing apparel"
- Sent as refugees into Georgia

## PART V: RESIGNATION AND EXILE (1781-1784)

### **May 1781**
**RESIGNATION:** Upon hearing of family's "distressed situation," Nicholas left regiment at Wilmington  
*Lacking any other means to support them*  
**Resigned commission for "trifling Sum of £150"**  
*For Nicholas (age ~41-43), military career over*  
*Family survival paramount*

### **Late 1781**
**Reunion in Georgia:** Nicholas joins family in Georgia (they had fled there late 1780)  
*Living as refugees within British lines*

### **c.1780-1782**
**ELIZABETH MOORE DIES:** Wife Elizabeth E. Moore dies sometime during this period  
*Likely during chaotic exile from North Carolina or in Georgia*  
*Critical evidence: 1782 St. Johns Town testimony describes Nicholas arriving "as a widower with six children"*

### **1782**
**Relocation to East Florida:** Nicholas moves family to **St. Johns Town** (also known as **Hester's Bluff**) on St. Johns River in East Florida  
*Joining thousands of Loyalist refugees evacuated from Savannah and Charleston*  
*Second most populous town in province almost overnight*  
*Developer Thomas Williamson platted 230 residential and commercial lots*

**St. Johns Town Holdings:**
- **A house in St. Johns Town**
- **A lot with a warehouse**
- **Nine enslaved laborers**
- **Tools and cattle** for agricultural operations
- **500-acre land grant from Governor Patrick Tonyn**

**East Florida Claims Commission Testimony:**  
**Nicholas Welsh** (spelling variant) arrived at St. Johns Town **"as a widower with six children"**

*Note: Description as "planter originally from New York" is error in testimony. Documented origins Pennsylvania and North Carolina. Confusion may have arisen from military service or temporary residence.*

### **1782-1783**
**St. Johns Town flourishing:** Settlement in "very flourishing way, convenient for trade"  
*River traffic suggested town of great consequence*

**Fellow resident John Russell testimony:**
> "the Town was in a very flourishing way, convenient for trade, and, from the extent of the river traffic must have become a Town of great consequence to the Country in a short time."

### **1783**
**Spain acquires Florida:** Britain cedes East Florida to Spain in Treaty of Paris  
**Loyalist refugees face another displacement**

### **1783-1784**
**Dismantling St. Johns Town:** Thomas Williamson dismantles personal dwelling, loads on barges, ships to Jamaica (1784)  
*Called sale price of £172 "the sole satisfaction or compensation" for his losses*

---

## PART VI: BAHAMAS REFUGE (1784-1786)

### **1784**
**Move to Bahamas:** Nicholas Welch, like most St. Johns Town residents, relocates to **New Providence, Bahamas**  
*For Nicholas (age ~44-46), third displacement in four years*

### **1784-1786**
**Life in exile:** Family lives as refugees in Bahamas  
*Dependent on government provisions for survival*

### **1784-1786**
**Two daughters marry:** Two of Nicholas's daughters married in Bahamas during this period

### **1786**
**Decision to petition:** Nicholas undertakes mission to London to petition Crown for compensation  
*One last mission on behalf of family*

---

## PART VII: THE LONDON MEMORIALS (1786)

### **20 March 1786**
**FIRST MEMORIAL FILED** - Memorial to the Lords Commissioners of His Majesty's Treasury  
*Carefully constructed narrative of victimhood*  
*Formal yet desperate appeal from ruined subject*

**Memorial details:**
- Unwavering loyalty to Crown
- Active military service as Major
- Immense suffering of family
- Detailed losses amounting to **£1,150 sterling**

**Conclusion of petition:**
> Half Pay... or some equivalent compensation, to enable him to return to his numerous & helpless family, who are now exposed to the greatest hardship, misery & want; without a dawn of hope for relief but from the Benevolence of His most Gracious Majesty and His Ministers.

### **October 1786**
**SECOND MEMORIAL FILED** - Additional petition to Crown

### **Late 1786**
**COMPENSATION AWARDED:** Crown awards Nicholas **£448 sterling**  
*Only 39% of claim (£448 of £1,150 requested)*  
*But enough to provide means for return*  
*Beginning of final, unexpected chapter*

### **Full Memorial text available:**  
http://www.royalprovincial.com/Military/mems/nc/clmwel.htm

## PART VIII: RETURN TO AMERICA (1786-1799)

### **c.1788**
**RETURN TO UNITED STATES** - Profound historical irony  
*Major Nicholas Welch — committed Loyalist who lost everything fighting against American cause*  
*Returns to very nation he opposed to build new life*  
*Nicholas (age ~48-50) back in enemy's country*

### **c.1788**
**South Carolina reappearance:** Appears in records in **Orangeburgh and 96 Districts, SC**  
*Far from political animosities of Carolinas*  
*Opportunity to begin again*

### **24 August 1789**
**Father's estate settled:** Estate sale of John Welch, dec'd  
*George Whitley and Margaret (widow of John Welch) Whitley of Lincoln Co, NC*  
*Other heirs including:*
- Nicholas Welch
- Thomas Welch
- William Welch
- Caleb Edmiston (Edmondson Jr.)
- Rebecca (Welch) Oliphant
- Thomas Martindell (Martindale)
*All of 96 District, SC, on deed in Lincoln Co, NC*

### **1790**
**Census:** Orangeburg District, SC (on South Edisto R.): **Nicholas Welch 1-2-3-0-0**  
*Listed next to Tory Captain John Salley Sr. (1740-1794), ancestor of great SC archivist A. S. Salley*  
*Nicholas (age ~50) re-establishing himself*

### **5 August 1793**
**LAND SPECULATOR:** Six land grants in South Carolina to Nicholas Welch  
**6 grants of 1,000 acres each = 6,000 total acres**

**Locations:**
- Charleston District (1,000 acres)
- Orangeburg District (2,000 acres - two grants)
- Pendleton or 96 District (3,000 acres - three grants)

*This helps tie Major Nicholas to 1790 Orangeburg census listing*  
*Former Tory now major American landholder*

### **1795**
**Land sale:** William Welch, Nicholas Welch and Thomas Welch of Pendleton District, SC, sell land to George Whitley (who married widow Margaret Welch) of Lincoln Co, NC  
*Part of estate of John Welch*

**Witnesses:**
- Josiah Underwood
- Thos. Martindel (Martindale who married Mary Welch)
- George Whitmore (whose sister Margaret married Nicholas Welch, son of Benjamin Welch)

### **26 December 1794**
**MARRIAGE #3:** **Nicholas Welsh** (alternate spelling) marries **SARAH FARREN** in **Philadelphia, Pennsylvania**  
*Nicholas (age ~54-56) marries Sarah (b.1751 VA, age ~43)*

**Questions about this marriage:**
- Was Nicholas in Philadelphia?
- Or traveling through?
- Was he already in Louisiana by this time?
- How did he meet Sarah Farren?
- Was this proxy marriage?

*Source: Pennsylvania, U.S., Compiled Marriage Records, 1700-1821*

**Move to Tennessee:** Nicholas and family relocate to **Giles County, Tennessee**

*Age approximately 74-76*

### **8 December 1814**
**PROBATE:** Giles County Court Minutes record:  
**"Ordered letters of adm. of est. of Nicholas WELCH, dec'd, issue to Sarah WELCH, she having qualified."**

**"Sarah WELCH, widow of the late Nicholas WELCH"**

## ANALYSIS: THE LIFE IN CONTEXT

### **Total Miles Traveled (estimated): 5,000+ miles**

**Major relocations:**
1. Lancaster Co, PA → Mecklenburg Co, NC (~400 miles)
2. NC → Briar Creek, GA (~250 miles)
3. GA → East Florida (~300 miles)
4. Florida → Bahamas (~400 miles by sea)
5. Bahamas → London, England (~4,000 miles by sea)
6. London → South Carolina (~4,000 miles by sea)
7. SC → Louisiana (~800 miles)
8. Louisiana → Tennessee (~600 miles)

**Total documented relocations: 8 major moves**

### **Years in Each Location:**
- Pennsylvania: 0-19 years (birth to ~1759)
- North Carolina: 13 years (1759-1781, with interruptions for war service)
- Georgia (brief): <1 year (1779 military, 1781 refuge)
- East Florida: 2 years (1782-1784)
- Bahamas: 2 years (1784-1786)
- London: <1 year (1786)
- South Carolina: 11 years (1788-1799)
- Louisiana: 11-13 years (1799-1811/12)
- Tennessee: 2-3 years (1812-1814)

### **Occupations/Roles:**
1. Farmer/Planter (NC, FL, LA, TN)
2. Landowner (NC - 470+ acres)
3. British Military Officer - MAJOR (1779-1781)
4. Recruiter for Loyalist cause (1779-1780)
5. Refugee (1781-1786)
6. Petitioner to Crown (1786)
7. Land Speculator (SC - 6,000 acres, 1793)
8. Tennessee Frontiersman (1812-1814)

### **Losses:**
- Mother (age ~2-4)
- First wife Elizabeth (age ~40-42, during war)
- 600 acres developed land in NC (confiscated)
- 2 slaves (confiscated)
- All personal property except clothing (confiscated)
- Military commission (resigned for £150)
- 500 acres in Florida (Spanish cession)
- House and warehouse in St. Johns Town (abandoned)

### **Compensations:**
- £448 from Crown (1786) - 39% of claim
- 6,000 acres in South Carolina (1793)
- Unknown holdings in Louisiana
- Property in Tennessee (1812-1814)

### **Imprisonments:**
1. Twice arrested for "Treason Against the State" (pre-1779, NC)
2. 34 days as POW in Greenwood, SC (March-April 1779)

### **Legal Costs:**
- £150 to secure release from treason charges
- Unknown costs for London petitions
- Legal fees for land transactions

### **Family Impact:**
- Wife and 9 children banished from NC (1780)
- Three sons captured at Ramsour's Mill (1780)
- Wife Elizabeth died during exile (c.1780-1782)
- Family scattered across South (16+ children)
- Two daughters married in Bahamas
- Multiple grandchildren across frontier

## THE HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE

### **Nicholas Welch as Representative Figure:**

1. **The Loyalist Experience:** Embodied the divisions of Revolution
2. **The Southern Strategy Failure:** His role at Ramsour's Mill demonstrated collapse of Cornwallis's plan
3. **Exile and Diaspora:** Representative of thousands of Loyalist refugees
4. **Reconciliation:** Return to US demonstrates healing after conflict
5. **Frontier Settlement:** Part of great western migration
6. **The American Story:** From colonial subject to exile to American pioneer
