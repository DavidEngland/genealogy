Excursus on Zachariah Welch

Here arises some question/debate as to whether Zacariah Welch of Anson County, North Carolina might actually be the son of Henry and Sarah Welch, of Chatham County, North Carolina; rather than of William Welchennelt, born in Ireland, occupation hatter; and his wife Mary (Queens) Welch, born in Scotland.

Entries found on Genforum and Ancestry.com-message board, both state that the Zachariah Welch who resided in Anson County, was born in Dublin, Ireland in 1772, and came to America with his parents when he was about six months old. However these statements made in print appear to be unproven; or at the least, have not been well documented by factual information that I have been able to find.

The apparent principle source for this statement pertaining to his place of birth comes from “The Tishomingo County Connection: History of Skinner-Welch * Adams- Seago and Allied Families of Tishomingo County, Mississippi”, compiled by Esther Welch Adams of River Heights, Crump, Tennessee (1955–1980), with a second printing in 1981. Its call number, 929.273 – Ad 25, is found in the Tishomingo County Archives in the old historic courthouse at Wadesboro.

According to Mrs. Adams’ manuscript, on page 269, she writes that he, meaning William Welch, “was said to have been a Welchenvelt, in Ireland.” Notice that it “was said” and thus not necessarily proved by sound documentation. There are no citations in her manuscript pertaining to a ship’s passenger list, extant bible records for births and marriages, or verifiable deeds, proven wills, etc., to substantiate that William Welch/Welchenvelt was married to Mary Queens, that they immigrated from Dublin, Ireland in 1772, and that “their ninth child Zachariah...” was born prior to the family’s migration to the Colonies or on board a ship, or that William and Mary Welch ever ventured into Anson County, North Carolina. This story may have been taken merely from family tradition, or strictly adapted from stories/legends handed down over generations; or possibly by conjecture.

Children Listed by Mrs. Adams

Mrs. Adams lists William and Mary (Queens) Welch’s children on page 269 as:

  • Jeremiah Welch, who died sometime after September 1, 1780
  • Mary Welch, born circa 1750, who remained unmarried
  • Augustus Welch, born circa 1752, killed in the Indian Wars
  • Roseannah Welch, born circa 1754, who married Richard Russell
  • Elizabeth Welch, who married William Benton
  • Catherine Welch, who married Henry Hunter
  • Sarah Welch, who married a Harris
  • Nancy Welch, who married a Laurence
  • Zachariah Welch, who married Priscilla Powell Carter

Comparison with the Children of Henry and Sarah Welch

A partition deed in Chatham County Deed Book “N”, page 229 (April 2, 1803) lists the heirs of Henry Welch Sr. and his widow Sarah:

  • Elijah Hunter and Catherine his wife
  • Elizabeth Welch
  • William Benton and Mary his wife
  • Henry Welch Jr.
  • Edmond Welch
  • Rosannah Welch
  • Sarah Welch
  • Jeremiah Welch
  • Nancy Welch
  • Zachariah Welch

The similarities between the two sets of children are striking. Eight names match directly: Zachariah, Jeremiah, Roseannah/Rosannah, Sarah, Nancy, Catherine, Elizabeth, and Mary. Three of these—Zachariah, Jeremiah, and Roseannah—are relatively uncommon for the period, strengthening the possibility of a connection.

Discrepancies and Questions

Mrs. Adams states that Mary Welch died unmarried, while Elizabeth married William Benton. However, the Chatham County deed identifies Mary (not Elizabeth) as the wife of William Benton. This raises the possibility that Mrs. Adams may have reversed the two sisters.

William Benton’s will (probated July 19, 1820, at Wadesborough) names children Jeremiah, William Henry, Rosanna Moore, Penelope White, Margaret Hunter Allen, and Sarah Harris Benton. These names again echo the Welch family naming patterns.

The Tombstone Question

The tombstone for Zachariah Welch lists his birth and death years and states that he was born in Dublin, Ireland. Mrs. Adams notes that no monuments existed until about 1970, when relatives placed them. Thus the inscription “Born in Dublin, Ireland” is not from an early stone and may reflect oral tradition rather than contemporary documentation.

Land Transactions and Geographic Clues

Several land transactions connect Henry Welch of Chatham County to Anson County, including a 1791 purchase near Gould’s Fork and a 1799 purchase of town lot No. 36 in Wadesborough. On August 15, 1823, Zachariah Welch sold this same lot to James Slaughter. No deed explains how Zachariah acquired it, but the chain of ownership suggests a connection to Henry of Chatham County.

Additional deeds show Henry and Jeremiah Welch witnessing transactions involving William Benton and John White—families also linked to the Chatham County Welches.

Other Welches in Anson County

By 1796, John Welch of Chatham County had moved to Anson County. His land was later sold for debts owed by his heirs: Polly, Patsey, Luke, John, Jane, Sally, and Edmund Welch. Henry and Sarah Welch also had a son named Edmund/Edmond, reinforcing the pattern.

The 1800 Anson County census lists John Welch, Henry Welch (likely Henry Jr.), and Zachariah Welch living in the same district.

Naming Patterns

Zachariah’s children include Edmund Lily, Roseannah Russell, Samuel Carter, Henry Hunter, Sarah Harris, Jeremiah Augustus White, and Nancy Welch. These names reflect the same cluster of surnames and given names found among the Chatham County Welches and their in-laws.

Absence of Evidence for William and Mary (Queens) Welch in Anson County

No records place William and Mary (Queens) Welch in Anson County. None of the siblings listed by Mrs. Adams appear there, except possibly Rosanna Russell and Elizabeth/Mary Benton—both of whom can also be linked to the Chatham County family.

Conclusion

While some uncertainties remain—particularly concerning the Hunter and Benton marriages and the “hatters” tradition—existing documentary evidence strongly favors identifying Zachariah Welch of Anson County as the son of Henry and Sarah Welch of Chatham County, rather than of William and Mary (Queens) Welch of Ireland and Scotland. Further primary-source research is needed to resolve the remaining inconsistencies.
