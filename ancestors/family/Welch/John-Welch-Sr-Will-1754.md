# Last Will and Testament of John Welch Sr.
## Paxton Township, Lancaster County, Pennsylvania
**Date:** January 21, 1754  
**Probated:** May 3, 1754  
**Testator:** John Welch Sr. (OCR shows "James Willsh" - likely error)  
**Executor:** Mary Welch (wife) and John Welch Jr. (son)

---

## Editorial Note

This transcription corrects OCR errors from the original 18th-century document. The testator's first name appears as "James Willsh" in the OCR, but evidence suggests this is **John Welch Sr.**, whose son **John Welch Jr.** (1712–1775) became the father of **Major Nicholas Welch** (c. 1738–1814), the Loyalist officer.

**Family Context:**
- **Testator:** John Welch Sr. (d. 1754), Paxton Township, Lancaster County, PA
- **Executor & Son:** John Welch Jr. (1712–1775) - married Elizabeth Whiteside (1738)
- **Grandson:** Nicholas Welch (c. 1738–1814) - would have been ~16 when this will was written

## Transcription

**Ex[ecutor] James Welsh** [*Editorial: Likely "John Welsh" - see note above*]

In the Name of God Amen.

I, James [*John?*] Welch of the Township of Paxton & County of Lancaster and Province of Pennsylvania, being very sick and weak of body, but of perfect mind and memory, and calling to mind the mortality of the body that it is appointed for all men once to die, and therefore I recommend my soul to God that gave it, and my body to the earth to be buried in a Christian and decent manner. And as for such worldly goods that God hath been pleased to bestow on me, I dispose of in the manner and form as followeth, viz:

**First:** I give and bequeath to my beloved wife **Mary Welch** all the household furniture, with one-third part of the cows and sheep, with one horse, and her maintenance out of the place during her widowhood or till the two youngest boys, viz. **Robert** and **Joseph**, come to age. And in case that she sees fit to alter her place of residence, she is to have **one hundred pounds** from my four youngest sons, viz. **Thomas**, **James**, **Robert**, and **Joseph**.

**Secondly:** I give and bequeath unto my son **John** forty pounds, to be paid in the year of our Lord 1759.

**Thirdly:** I give and bequeath to my son-in-law **William Peterson** ten pounds, to be paid in the year 1759.

**Fourthly:** I give and bequeath unto my son **Thomas** one hundred and fifty acres of land from the middle of the Great Swamp and backward to the east corner, and one horse named Buck.

**Fifthly:** I give and bequeath unto my son **James** one hundred acres [*OCR: "Excors"*] of land from the middle of the Great Swamp & backwards, joining James McKnaught.

**Sixthly:** I give and bequeath to my two sons **Robert** and **Joseph** the remainder of the land next the river.

**Seventhly:** I give and bequeath to my daughter **Jean** one bay horse with a large blaze in his forehead, with a side saddle and bridle, with forty pounds of money to be paid in the year 1759.

**Eighthly:** I give and bequeath to my daughter **Ezabel** [*Isabel/Elizabeth*] fifty pounds when [she] come[s] to age.

**Ninthly:** I give and bequeath to my daughter **Mary** fifty pounds when she come[s] to age.

**N.B.** [*Nota Bene*]: That these four sons - **Thomas**, **James**, **Robert**, and **Joseph** - is to pay all debts and legacies according as each of them possesses of the land.

And I order and appoint my well-beloved wife **Mary Welch** and son **John Welch** my executors, revoking all other wills and executors whatsoever.

As witness my hand and seal this **21st day of January, 1754**.

I order the servant man **Michel McDaniel** for the benefit of the plantation, and **Thomas** to be his master. And I order that the two youngest [sons] have their victuals [*food*] and clothes until they be fit to do for themselves.

**Signed:** James [*John?*] **J. Welch** [seal: L.S.]

**Witnesses:**
- Richard McClure
- William Anderson

## Probate Certificate

The 3rd day of May, 1754, before me the subscriber came **Richard McClure** and **William Anderson**, the witnesses to the within written will, and on their corporal [oath] did declare and said that they were present and saw and heard **James [John?] Welch**, the testator, sign, seal, publish, and declare the within writing as and for his last will and testament, and that at the doing thereof he was of sound and disposing mind, memory, and understanding, according to the best of their knowledge, observation, and belief.

**Signed:** Edw[ard] Shippen, D[eputy] M[aster]

## Historical Context

**Date Context:** January 1754
- Seven Years' War beginning (1754-1763)
- Pennsylvania still under proprietary government of the Penn family
- Paxton Township in Lancaster County was frontier settlement area
- Scotch-Irish Presbyterian immigration wave to Pennsylvania backcountry

**Currency:** Pounds referenced are Pennsylvania pounds (colonial currency)

**Legal Context:**
- Widows typically received one-third of estate (dower rights)
- Eldest son John receives monetary bequest (40 pounds) rather than land - suggests he was already established
- Youngest sons Robert and Joseph to be maintained until age - typical for minors
- Daughters receive monetary bequests "at age" (typically 18 or marriage)

## Connection to Major Nicholas Welch (Welch-185)

If **John Welch** named as executor is indeed **John Welch Jr.** (1712-1775):

**Timeline:**
- **1754:** This will written/probated - John Welch Jr. age ~42, named executor
- **1738:** John Welch Jr. married Elizabeth Whiteside (Feb 6, 1738)
- **c. 1738-1740:** Nicholas Welch born to John Jr. and Elizabeth
- **1742:** Elizabeth Whiteside died - Nicholas age ~4
- **1754:** This will - Nicholas age ~16, grandson of testator
- **1769:** John Welch Jr. died in Tryon County, NC - Nicholas granted letters testamentary
- **1814:** Nicholas Welch died in Giles County, TN

**Migration Pattern:**
- **1754:** John Welch Sr. in Paxton Township, Lancaster County, PA (this will)
- **By 1760s:** John Welch Jr. migrated to Tryon County, North Carolina (with son Nicholas)
- **1769:** John Jr. died in NC, Nicholas administered estate
- **1772:** Nicholas received 300-acre land grant on Indian Creek, NC
- **1780-1814:** Nicholas's Revolutionary War service, exile, return - see [Welch-185.md](Welch-185.md)
