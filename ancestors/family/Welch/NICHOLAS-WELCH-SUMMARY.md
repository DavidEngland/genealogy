# Nicholas Welch Family: Quick Reference Guide

Between 1725 and 1860, at least **eleven men named Nicholas Welch** lived and left records across the American South. This guide provides quick identification to prevent confusion in genealogical research.

## The Major Lines: Direct Descendants

### LINE A: Major Nicholas Welch Sr. (#1) - THE LOYALIST
**Born:** c.1740 Lancaster Co, PA  
**Died:** Dec 1814 Giles Co, TN  
**Father:** John Welch (1716-1769)  
**Grandfather:** William Welsh (d. c.1748)

**The Epic Journey:**
- Pennsylvania → North Carolina → Prisoner of War → British Officer
- Savannah → East Florida → Bahamas → London → South Carolina
- Louisiana → Tennessee

**Military Rank:** MAJOR, Hamilton's Regiment, Royal British Army of North Carolina

**Marriages:**
1. Unknown wife #1 (possibly)
2. **Elizabeth E. Moore** (c.1740 - c.1780-1782) - died during Revolutionary War exile
3. **Sarah Farren** (b.1751 VA, m.1794 Philadelphia, survived him as widow 1814)

**Children:** 16+ documented, including John, William C., Judith, Nicholas William, Mary, Margaret E., Thomas (1st), Sarah, Ann, Hester (1st), John (2nd), Richard, Hester (2nd), Thomas (2nd), Elizabeth, Lewis

**Key Historical Events:**
- Battle of Ramsour's Mill (June 20, 1780)
- St. Johns Town, East Florida (1782-1784) - arrived as widower with 6 children
- Memorial to the Crown (London, March 1786)
- Settlement in Louisiana (1799-1810)
- Final residence Tennessee (1812-1814)

**WikiTree:** [Welch-185](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Welch-185)

## Military Service Summary

### Revolutionary War - LOYALIST (British) Side

**Major Nicholas Welch Sr. (#1)**
- **Rank:** MAJOR
- **Unit:** Hamilton's Regiment, Royal British Army of North Carolina (also called Royal No. Carolina Regiment)
- **Service:** 1778-1781
- **Key Actions:**
  - Recruited 270 Loyalists for British service
  - March to Briar Creek, Georgia (February 1779) - 50+ casualties
  - Joined Colonel Archibald Campbell's army
  - Battle of Ramsour's Mill (June 20, 1780) - DEFEAT
  - Three sons captured at Ramsour's Mill
- **POW Status:** 10 Mar to 12 Apr 1779 - 34 days imprisoned in Greenwood, SC (96 District) with brothers William and Thomas
- **Resignation:** May 1781 - resigned commission for £150 to rejoin exiled family in Savannah
- **Memorial Claims:** Filed 1786 in London, awarded £448 compensation

**Brothers' Service:**
- **William Welch:** Initially Loyalist POW (1779), later switched sides and took oath to Patriots
- **Thomas Welch:** Initially Loyalist POW (1779), later switched sides and took oath to Patriots

### Revolutionary War - PATRIOT Side

**William Welch (brother of David & John Welch)**
- Filed successful claim for losses during Revolution
- Claim APPROVED because he was a Patriot
- Distinguished from his Tory uncle William Welch from Lincoln Co, NC

### French and Indian War

**Benjamin Welch (father of Nicholas #5)**
- **Service:** 1760
- **Unit:** Virginia Regiment
- Brother Daniel Welch also served

### War of 1812
- Nicholas Welch Sr. on 1812 Giles County, TN tax list (age 72)
- Nicholas #7 lived through War of 1812, Civil War, Reconstruction

## The "Two Elizabeths, Both Moores" Mystery

**Critical Research Question:** Did Major Nicholas Welch (#1) marry two different women, both named Elizabeth Moore?

### Theory: Sequential Marriages to Two Elizabeths

**Evidence For:**
1. **St. Johns Town Testimony (1782):** Nicholas arrived in Florida "as a widower with six children"
2. **FindAGrave Memorial 234100609:** Lists Elizabeth Moore death as "1799" 
3. **Different parentage theories:** Some sources say John William Moore, others say Moses Moore
4. **Children timeline:** Early children (1758-1762) vs. late children (1765-1780) suggest two mothers

**DNA Testing Strategy:**
- Compare autosomal DNA of descendants from early children (pre-1762) vs. late children (post-1765)
- If two different Elizabeths: descendants should match different Moore family branches
- mtDNA testing of female-line descendants can definitively prove maternal lineage

**Current Status:**
- DNA-RESEARCH-STRATEGY.md provides comprehensive testing protocol
- WikiTree has 9 known DNA testers in Welch family
- Testing would resolve: two wives vs. one wife, Moore parentage, Sarah confusion

### The "Sarah Confusion"

**Question:** Was Sarah Farren (m.1794 Philadelphia) the same person as Sarah Welch administering the 1814 estate?

**Scenarios:**
- A) Sarah Farren married Nicholas, survived as widow, administered estate (standard interpretation)
- B) Daughter Sarah Welch (b.1766) administered estate of her father
- C) Both involved: widow + daughter both named Sarah
