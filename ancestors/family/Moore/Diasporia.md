Following the **Revolutionary War**, the **Moore family** underwent a significant diaspora that diverted from the path taken by Major Nicholas Welch, specifically opting for **Spanish West Florida** over the Bahamas.

### **Moses Moore’s Migration and Spanish Land Grant**
Unlike Nicholas Welch, who fled to the Bahamas and London to seek restitution, **Moses Moore (c. 1718–1791)** moved his household to the **Mobile District** of Spanish West Florida to escape persecution as a Loyalist. This move was strategically centered on acquiring land under the Spanish Crown, which had regained control of the Floridas following the **Treaty of Paris (1783)**.

*   **Valuation and Acquisition:** On **October 1, 1787**, Moses Moore petitioned the Governor General of Louisiana for a tract of land on the **Tombigbee River**. This property, approximately **800 acres**, had previously belonged to a British commissary named McIntosh but was considered vacant after McIntosh evacuated in 1780.
*   **The Sunflower Plantation:** The grant was approved on **October 22, 1787**, by Estevan Miro, and the plantation was located on the west side of the river near the modern-day town of **Sunflower, Alabama**.
*   **Shifting Jurisdictions:** This region changed hands several times, transitioning from British to Spanish rule before being absorbed into the **Mississippi Territory** of the United States following the **Louisiana Purchase (1803)** and the subsequent **Adams-Onís Treaty**.
*   **Value of the Grant:** These Spanish grants were extremely valuable because they provided a legal basis for land claims under the American government; Moses’s daughter, **Ann Lawrence**, successfully obtained a patent for the 800 acres from the U.S. Board of Commissioners in **1804** based on her father's right of occupancy.

### **The Moore and Welch "Party"**
The sources indicate that Moses Moore was accompanied to West Florida by his daughter **Ann** and her husband **Joseph Lawrence**. There is strong circumstantial evidence regarding the dispersal of Nicholas Welch's children into this "western party":

*   **Dispersal of the Welch Children:** In his **1786 memorial**, Nicholas Welch explicitly stated that while he was in London, his children were "**dispersed... among such families as could be prevailed on to take them in**".
*   **Nicholas Welch Jr. (b. 1762):** Documentation suggests that the older son, **Nicholas William Welch**, became the "man of the family" on the mainland. He did not reunite with his father in the Bahamas but remained in the **South Carolina/Georgia/Alabama** area, marrying **Margaret Hembree**.
*   **The Older Sons:** Records show that Nicholas Welch's sons **William and Thomas** also remained on the mainland, eventually renouncing the Crown and taking an oath to the Patriot cause in South Carolina while their father was still in exile.

### **The Geographic Divergence**
The diaspora represents two distinct Loyalist survival strategies. While the Welch patriarch focused on **maritime exile** (Bahamas/London) to plead for government compensation, the Moore patriarch and the younger Welch generation pursued **frontier relocation**. By moving west into the Tombigbee region, this party placed themselves in a territory where the British and Spanish boundaries were constantly in flux (specifically the **31st parallel vs. the 32° 28' line**), allowing them to rehabilitate their social standing as pioneering planters as the region was eventually annexed by the United States.
