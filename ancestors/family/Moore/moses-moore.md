Moses Moore, the immigrant, and father of the notorious Tory, Col. John Moore, who lived on Indian Creek in Lincoln County, North Carolina(present-day Gaston County, North Carolina), was born ca 1718 in Carlisle, England. He came to America about 1745. He was the father of Aaron Moore, Sarah Moore (Logan), Hester Moore (Roberts) and Col. John Moore "The Tory."

The following biographical sketch of Moses Moore appears in the book History of Old Tryon and Rutherford Counties, North Carolina ... 1730-1936 by Clarence W. Griffin17.
"Moses Moore was a native of Carlisle, England, and migrated to Virginia in 1745. He married a Miss Winston, of near Jamestwon, VA. He migrated to North Carolina about 1753, and settled in what is now Gaston County, eight miles west of Lincolnton. He was a large landowner and a hunter, and farmer by profession. He was a signer of the Tryon Association, but apparently took no active part in the Revolution, no doubt on account of his age, but his sympathies were generally British. One of his sons, Col. John Moore, who was born in Gaston County, was educated in Granville County, N.C. When the Revolution broke out John Moore became a zealous Loyalist, and led a part of Tories from Tryon County, N.C. in February, 1779, to Georgia, and uniting with Col. Boyd on the way, they were defeated at Kettle Creek by Col. Pickens. He is said to have participated in the defense of Savannah, and was in service of the British a number of times after this. He was a Lieutenant-Colonel in Hamilton's Corps of Loyalists, and was the commanding officer of the British at the Battle of Ramsour's Mill."

After the Revolutionary War he left North Carolina and moved to what was then the Province of West Florida, District of Mobile (today Washington County, Alabama). Nothing is known about his activities from 1783 when he arrived in South Florida, until 1787 at which time he petitioned the Spanish authorities for a grant of land of about 800 acres. This land had originally been granted to a John McIntosh in 1775 when the British had control of that area. In 1780 McIntosh had apparently abandoned it after Spain had again gained control. The original land grant is in Spanish.
On 22 October 1787 the grant was approved and it is assumed that Moses Moore took possession and commenced farming. Moses Moore was a "Planter," and the plantation was located on the west side of the Tombigbee, River, near the small town of Sunflower, Alabama. The history of this area is very unclear and almost nothing again is known about Moses Moore and his farm until in 1797 when it appears the heirs of John McIntosh attempted to reclaim the plantation. According to the research of James Havilah Gordon, there were several hearings, etc. and in 1804 Ann Lawrence who, as the representative for Moses Moore, was approved as the owner "by right of occupancy," on 28 March 1804, by a Board of Commissioners. From this, it is assumed that Moses Moore died sometime between 1797 and 1800 and Ann Lawrence had been designated as his heir.

Sally Randol-Hardy states that the book "Pioneers of Washington County, Alabama" has the death of Moses Moore as 1791.
